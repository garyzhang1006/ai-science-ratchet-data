\begin{abstract}
Scientific claims now reach many of their readers through language models, and repeated LLM processing degrades information in general text. Single-step LLM summaries of science already overgeneralize, but whether repeated summarization drifts in a direction, and where along the chain the loss falls, are unmeasured. We preregistered the prediction that summarization chains push claims toward certainty, with fewer hedges and harder causal language, and then measured it on 360 chains of depth ten built from 60 stratified PubMed abstracts, three open-weight models, and two prompt regimes. We find that hedge density rises by $0.061$ per 100 words per generation ($p = 6.9 \times 10^{-8}$), against the preregistered direction, and the reversal holds in all three models with no sign flips, while causal strength doesn't move ($p = 0.70$). Precision falls instead, as exact retention of effect sizes, intervals, and p-values drops $0.055$ per generation and qualifier retention drops $0.049$ per generation, both at $p < 10^{-39}$, and claims end up less checkable. No marker we measure moves toward overstatement, though a third of our sources already sit at the top of the causal scale, where hardening can't register. Most of the loss occurs early, and one summarization removes $44\%$ of a paper's reported statistics, a loss that is $80\%$ of the total over ten generations. The depth of a summarization chain therefore matters much less than whether the text passed through a summarizer even once. A prompt that tells the model to keep every hedge, qualifier, and number cuts the numeric loss rate by $81\%$, though those summaries run $1.6$ times as long as neutral ones, and about half of the retention gain survives inside the length band the two regimes share. Our preregistered survival test returns the same clean null from a saturated entailment scorer and, once we repair the scorer, from a real first-hop floor effect, and only a self-consistency check tells those two cases apart.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A scientific finding now often passes through a language model, and sometimes through several, before it reaches the person who acts on it. \citet{mohamed2025telephone} measured what this costs on news and narrative text, where facts mutate and information degrades under repeated processing. A single LLM summary already broadens a study's conclusions beyond its evidence, as \citet{peters2025generalization} showed. The closest work on direction is \citet{belem2026certainty}, who measured expressed certainty rising when a model rewrites its own text over many rounds, though their chains rewrite at length and don't compress. None of this work asks whether the loss on scientific claims runs in a particular direction under repeated summarization, or where along the chain it falls. We ask whether repeated AI summarization strips hedges, hardens causal language, and erases null results faster than unbiased noise would.

A consistent push in one direction is a ratchet, in which each hop keeps what the last one took and a per-generation bias that looks negligible compounds over the several hops a real citation chain runs, and we preregistered that ratchet on two grounds, that \citet{bartlett1932remembering} showed that human serial reproduction sharpens material toward schema-congruent forms, and that instruction-tuned models learn from preferences that reward confident prose.

The data didn't support that prediction, and hedge density per 100 words instead increases under repeated summarization in all three models we tested, and causal strength doesn't change. The models remove numbers and qualifiers at each generation, and precision falls with them.

The shape of the loss over depth also differed from our expectation. Degradation is \emph{front-loaded}, most of the numeric loss lands on the first summarization, and the curve flattens after that.

We measure the \emph{direction} of LLM summarization drift on scientific claims, under preregistered hypotheses and deterministic scoring that needs no human annotation. We also report a failure of our own, in which our preregistered test returned a clean survival curve that meant nothing because the NLI scorer marked almost nothing as supported once we fed it document-length premises. The repair is known~\citep{laban2022summac}, and what we add is the self-consistency check that detects the failure, together with a second NLI model that reproduces the corrected scores.

\section{Related work}

Iterative generation distorts text in measurable ways, and \citet{mohamed2025telephone} ran chains of translation and rephrasing and documented cumulative factual drift, while \citet{shumailov2024collapse} showed the training-side analog, model collapse when models train on the output of earlier models. \citet{belem2026certainty} measured expressed certainty under repeated LM rewriting and found it rising, the direction we preregistered and didn't see, though their chains rewrite at length while ours summarize. Serial reproduction in humans, from Bartlett's original experiments onward~\citep{bartlett1932remembering} to the iterated-learning laboratory of \citet{kirby2008cumulative}, shows systematic conventionalization and sharpening, and \citet{acerbi2023biases} ran that paradigm through an LLM and recovered the same content biases that human chains show. We score scientific claims with deterministic instruments in place of human annotators, and our result runs the opposite way under summarization, because human serial reproduction increases the material's definiteness and repeated summarization decreases it.

On the science-communication side, \citet{sumner2014exaggeration} found that exaggerated causal claims in health news track exaggeration already present in the academic press release, and \citet{yavchitz2012misrepresentation} traced spin in press releases to the trial's own abstract. Closest to our question, \citet{peters2025generalization} compared 4{,}900 single-step LLM summaries with their sources and found that most models broadened the scope of the conclusions, close to five times as often as human summaries did. Our qualifier loss measures that broadening over a chain, where a dropped population or dose that once bounded a claim widens its scope. Hedging has a shared task in NLP~\citep{farkas2010conll}, and we take our hedge list straight from \citet{hyland2005metadiscourse}, while \citet{liang2024mapping} and \citet{kobak2024delving} estimated that LLMs already touch a substantial share of scientific text.

\section{Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Source claims and stratification}

We drew 60 abstracts from the PubMed open-access subset and stratified them into three claim classes of 20 abstracts each. These are randomized trials whose abstracts report at least one numeric effect size with an interval, observational studies with unnegated association language, and abstracts reporting an explicit null result. A null result means a null phrase (``no significant difference'', ``did not differ'', ``was not associated'', and similar) appearing in the final third of the abstract. Each abstract takes one class only, and a null result takes priority over the other two classes, and we class an abstract as null whenever its conclusion reports a null result. Without that rule, fetch order absorbs null-result trials into the trial class, and the null class shrinks without any warning. We keep abstracts of 150--450 words, a gate fixed in the preregistration.

\subsection{Chains and regimes}

For each abstract, we generate chains of depth ten under every combination of three models (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Phi-3.5-mini-instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3) and two prompt regimes. The \emph{neutral} prompt reads, in full, ``Summarize the following scientific text faithfully, at approximately its original length.'' The \emph{conservative} prompt appends ``Preserve every hedge, qualifier, number, and statement of uncertainty exactly as expressed; do not state any claim more strongly than the original does.'' This gives 360 chains, 180 per regime, and 3{,}600 scored generations. The generation budget is $1.5$ times the input length, capped at 1{,}024 tokens, and a summary can run longer than its source before the cap binds. Generation and hop mean the same thing throughout, one summarization step, and we index the unsummarized source as generation zero. Decoding is greedy, to separate systematic drift from sampling variance, and a sensitivity arm reruns a stratified 20-abstract subset through all three models under the neutral prompt at temperature $0.7$ with a fixed seed, which adds 60 chains and 600 further generations. We ran every chain on a single Kaggle T4 with 4-bit NF4 quantization, at 4.1 to 7.0 hours per model.\footnote{We preregistered Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct as the third model, but the Hugging Face token for that gated model never reached the Kaggle kernel, because an account-level secret isn't attached to a notebook unless the notebook opts in, so the harness fell back to Phi-3.5-mini-instruct as its fallback table specifies. We noticed the substitution only after the Phi run had finished, and kept the ungated set, which needs no gated-model token to replicate.}

\subsection{Instruments}

Five instruments score every generation, all deterministic given the code in the archive of Section~\ref{sec:repro}, and we call the quantity each instrument returns a marker. Hedge density counts matches against a 101-item hedge list drawn from the hedges category of Hyland's metadiscourse appendix~\citep{hyland2005metadiscourse}, normalized per 100 words. The causal-strength scale runs in five steps from no-relation to unqualified causation, and we apply it to the strongest claim sentence. We scope negation to clause boundaries, so that a safety clause such as ``there were no dropouts'' can't invert the efficacy claim that follows it. A negation demotes the sentence instead of getting its own category, so ``did not reduce mortality'' scores $2$, the level an unnegated association also receives, and a chain that drops a negation for an association leaves this scale unchanged. For numeric fidelity, we extract effect sizes, confidence intervals, p-values, and percentages by pattern and score them for exact retention against the source, and we exclude the confidence level itself so that preserving the boilerplate ``95\% CI'' earns no credit. The qualifier instrument takes population phrases, dosages, and study-design terms from the source, and we count one as retained when at least $60\%$ of its tokens appear anywhere in the generation, and a single-token qualifier must match in full. That rule ignores word order and scope, so a summary keeping ``adults'' and ``diabetes'' while dropping the phrase that binds them still earns credit, which makes measured qualifier loss a lower bound. Entailment, which tracks summary faithfulness better than overlap metrics do~\citep{maynez2020faithfulness}, scores each generation against the source in both directions with \texttt{cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-base}, a public natural language inference (NLI) model. The same model checks whether the source's strongest claim sentence survives in each generation, a check we call core-finding entailment. That last instrument produced a false result on its first run, and Section~\ref{sec:validation} reports what went wrong. A positive control confirms that the causal-strength and hedge-density instruments move when the text does. A rewrite of every associational phrase in the 60 sources into unqualified causation lifts mean causal strength from $3.07$ to $4.23$, and $43\%$ of abstracts move up with none moving the wrong way. Hedging the same sentences instead drops the mean to $2.62$, where only $18\%$ of abstracts move, so the scale registers strengthening more often than it registers hedging. Those rates follow from where the sources sit, and $33\%$ of the abstracts already score at the ceiling of five, which leaves the rewrite no room to move them.

\subsection{Analysis}

The estimand for H1 is the mean per-generation change in each marker. We specified a mixed model with a random intercept per source abstract and a cluster-robust ordinary least squares (OLS) fallback. In practice, the fallback fired in every main-arm cell, because differencing removes the abstract-level intercept and leaves almost no level variance for a random intercept to absorb, which the data confirm, where the intraclass correlation across source abstracts runs from $0.27$ to $0.58$ on the levels and from $0.005$ to $0.023$ on the differences we model, which Appendix~\ref{app:design} verifies against the score tables. Main-arm standard errors are therefore clustered on the source abstract (60 clusters), which carries the dependence among the ten differences within a chain, and all tests are two-sided. The 3{,}600 main-arm generations rest on 60 independent source abstracts, and the sample size that limits every interval here is 60 and not the generation count. Most markers have 1{,}800 such differences per regime, numeric fidelity has 1{,}560, since we drop abstracts with no extractable statistic, and for qualifier retention 1{,}710. We Holm-correct across the five markers within each regime, so a corrected p-value holds within a regime and not across the two. The preregistration names three hypotheses, of which H1 says every marker drifts toward certainty, H2 says the core finding of a null-result abstract erodes faster than that of a non-null one, and H3 says conservative prompting shrinks every rate without flipping a sign. We fixed all hypotheses, model specifications, and exclusion rules in a time-stamped preregistration in that archive.

\subsection{Drift runs toward vagueness}

Hedge density rises by $0.061$ per 100 words per generation under neutral prompting (95\% CI $0.039$ to $0.083$, $p = 6.9 \times 10^{-8}$), the opposite of the preregistered direction, while causal strength doesn't move ($+0.0067$, $p = 0.70$). The interval on that estimate runs from $-0.027$ to $+0.040$ on the five-step scale, which bounds any hardening at $0.40$ scale points across a whole ten-generation chain, or $35\%$ of what rewriting every association as unqualified causation does to the same abstracts. Table~\ref{tab:h1} gives the per-generation effect for every marker in each regime, and the causal-strength scale takes the strongest sentence in a text, which makes it report a ceiling and miss any loosening around that sentence. That bound describes the reach of the scale more than the behavior of the text, because the positive control moved only a minority of abstracts under deliberate rewriting in either direction. An instrument that fires on a minority of deliberate rewrites attenuates any real drift toward zero, so this null puts a tight bound on measured hardening and a far looser bound on true overstatement, which leaves the precision losses below, all of them positive findings, as the evidence this paper rests on. Over ten generations, mean hedge density increases from $0.72$ to $1.33$ per 100 words, an $85\%$ increase, and neither marker behaves as a certainty ratchet requires.

The rise is in the share of words that are hedges, while the raw count per summary is flat, at $-0.018$ per generation ($p = 0.25$), while neutral summaries shrink from 210 words to 116 by generation ten, and the hedges that survive fill a larger share of a smaller text. The models shed numbers and qualifiers faster than they shed hedges, which raised the question of whether compression alone explains the rise, and regressing the per-generation change in density on the per-generation change in word count leaves a residual density increase of $+0.045$ per generation ($p < 10^{-3}$), which rules that out. We get the same answer normalizing by sentences instead of by words, at $+0.0120$ hedges per sentence per generation ($p = 7 \times 10^{-9}$). 

In a post hoc check, the rise isn't a matter of approximators or frequency words either. Hyland's list includes approximators such as ``about'' and ``approximately'', and frequency words such as ``often''. With every item of that kind dropped, the density of epistemic hedges (``may'', ``could'', ``suggests'', ``indicates'') still rises from $0.51$ to $0.91$ per 100 words, $+0.040$ per generation ($p = 1 \times 10^{-5}$).

Within each model, hedge density rises, with no sign flip in any of the three, and the uncorrected per-model estimates run Qwen2.5-7B $+0.0499$ ($p = 4.0 \times 10^{-4}$), Phi-3.5-mini $+0.0969$ ($p = 3.1 \times 10^{-6}$), and Mistral-7B $+0.0366$ ($p = 2.0 \times 10^{-4}$), and the smallest model, Phi, shows the largest rise.

The marker that erodes is precision, where the preregistration expected certainty to harden. Numeric fidelity, meaning exact retention of effect sizes, intervals, and p-values, declines $0.055$ per generation, qualifier retention declines $0.049$, and bidirectional entailment against the source falls $0.068$, all at $p < 10^{-39}$. The entailment term also falls for pure compression, and Section~\ref{sec:validation} therefore separates the term's forward-only component. In the neutral regime, pooled numeric fidelity drops from $1.00$ to $0.45$ and qualifier retention from $0.98$ to $0.49$ across ten generations.

\subsection{The first hop does most of the damage}

Figure~\ref{fig:drift} shows a distribution of loss over depth that we didn't anticipate. Numeric fidelity falls from $1.00$ to $0.56$ between the source and generation one, a loss that strips $44\%$ of the original statistics and accounts for $80\%$ of all numeric loss across the full ten generations. We see the same shape in qualifier retention, where the first hop accounts for $52\%$ of total loss. The curves then flatten, and numeric fidelity falls only from $0.56$ to $0.45$ across the remaining nine generations.

This shape matters more for intervention design than the direction result does. Under exponential compounding, the correct intervention would be to shorten the chain. Cutting a chain from four hops to two moves numeric fidelity from $0.49$ to $0.52$, because most of the numbers were gone after the first hop. The word ratchet in our title therefore names irreversibility, since pooled numeric fidelity and qualifier retention never recover at a later hop, and the compounding our preregistration assumed doesn't appear.

\subsection{Conservative prompting reduces four of five drift rates}
\label{sec:h3}

Under the instruction to preserve every hedge, qualifier, number, and statement of uncertainty, four of the five drift rates shrink and none flips sign, and H3 holds for those four. Causal strength, null in both regimes, drifts further under the conservative prompt, and that difference is itself null ($p = 0.66$). Numeric loss falls by $81.1\%$ ($p = 7.6 \times 10^{-42}$ on the interaction), qualifier loss by $76.9\%$ ($p = 1.6 \times 10^{-27}$), entailment loss by $58.9\%$ ($p = 3.3 \times 10^{-117}$), and the hedge-density rise shrinks by $45.7\%$ ($p = 0.012$ raw, $0.024$ after Holm), so all four survive Holm correction. At generation ten, conservative prompting retains numeric fidelity of $0.90$ against $0.45$ under neutral, and qualifier retention of $0.86$ against $0.49$. The protection reaches the finding itself, where pooled core-finding entailment at generation ten stands at $0.73$ under conservative prompting against $0.30$ under neutral.

The models pay for that protection in length, and conservative summaries average 195 words across the chain against 123 for neutral ones, from a 210-word source. Length therefore competes with the instruction as an explanation of the whole result, since both retention instruments credit retained material, and we compare the regimes inside the band of realized lengths they share. Holding the source abstract fixed, the numeric gap falls from $0.42$ to $0.23$, the qualifier gap from $0.32$ to $0.17$, and the forward-entailment gap from $0.27$ to $0.08$, with all three still above zero at $95\%$ confidence. That restriction is observational, with no length-matched trial behind it, so it bounds the confound without removing it, and Appendix~\ref{app:length} gives the intervals.

\subsection{Null results erode fastest under the continuous test}

The preregistered H2 test is the first generation at which core-finding entailment drops below $0.5$, censored at generation ten and compared across claim classes by log-rank, and it is null in both regimes (neutral $p = 0.40$, conservative $p = 0.21$). Figure~\ref{fig:halflife} shows why the binary test has little left to discriminate, since $57\%$ of neutral chains cross the threshold at the first hop, which puts the median survival time at a single generation in every class and leaves the log-rank comparison little room to separate them. Only 52 of the 180 conservative chains ever cross the threshold and $71\%$ are censored at generation ten, so the conservative null fails for want of events, where the neutral test failed from saturation. In its continuous form the same question does discriminate, where we regress core-finding entailment on generation interacted with a null-result indicator, over all eleven generations of the neutral regime, with errors clustered on abstract. The baseline decay is $-0.032$ per generation ($p < 10^{-14}$), and null-result abstracts lose a further $-0.0165$ per generation ($p = 0.009$). Null-result abstracts lose their core finding about $1.5$ times as fast as non-null ones, the direction H2 predicted. The gap persists after the first hop, and when we restrict the sample to generations one through ten, the baseline decay is $-0.0078$ ($p = 0.048$) and the null-result excess $-0.0145$ ($p = 0.041$), though that restricted excess is the one exploratory result here that doesn't survive a Holm correction applied across all thirteen exploratory tests we report, which Appendix~\ref{app:multiplicity} sets out in full. Class trajectories bear this out, with null-result core findings falling from $0.97$ to $0.21$ over ten generations, against $0.37$ for observational and $0.32$ for trial abstracts. We didn't preregister this test, and the p-values we quote for it in this section are uncorrected, so it deserves replication before anyone leans on it. For a null-result abstract, the strongest claim sentence is in most cases not the null statement itself, so this test tracks the strongest surviving claim. Appendix~\ref{app:case} follows one null-result chain generation by generation, and shows the evidence leaving before the conclusion does.


\subsection{Temperature sensitivity}

We reran a stratified 20-abstract subset through all three models under the same neutral prompt at temperature $0.7$, and the results reproduce the sign of all four rule-based markers and most of their magnitude. The primary scorer never ran on this arm, which leaves entailment with no sensitivity estimate, and the numeric cell here is the one place where the mixed model converged and the clustered fallback didn't fire. Hedge density drifts $+0.0515$ against $+0.0527$ for greedy decoding on the same abstracts, numeric fidelity $-0.0610$ against $-0.0483$, and qualifier retention $-0.0546$ against $-0.0443$, with the two information-loss estimates larger under sampling, though we ran no test of that difference on 20 abstracts, and causal strength is null in both. After Holm correction within that arm's four markers, hedge density keeps its effect size and loses significance ($p = 0.09$).

\subsection{From per-generation rates to what readers receive}
Readers meet these claims two or three citation hops from the source, well short of ten, and Appendix~\ref{app:depth} composes the measured neutral trajectories along a citation-depth profile from OpenAlex~\citep{priem2022openalex}. That composition assumes each citation hop is a summarization event, which our data never observes, so we keep it out of the results proper and treat it as an illustration of what the measured rates would imply at realistic depths.

\section{Instrument validation and a spurious null}
\label{sec:validation}

Our first entailment run produced a clean H2 result, a median survival time of one generation in every claim class in both regimes, and it was an artifact.

The check that caught it asks whether a source abstract entails a sentence copied out of that same abstract. Each source scored against its own core-finding sentence gave a mean entailment of $0.056$, far below the value near one that this quantity must take. The instrument was reporting almost nothing as supported, and most chains therefore crossed the erosion threshold at the first hop and the classes became indistinguishable by construction. Truncation doesn't explain it, because the median premise is 287 tokens against the model's 512-token limit. The cause was the model itself, a sentence-pair NLI cross-encoder trained on single-sentence premises, which mislabels document-length ones.

We followed \citet{laban2022summac} and replaced whole-document scoring with sentence-level NLI aggregation, which compares each hypothesis sentence against every premise sentence and keeps the best-supporting one, and that change raised self-entailment to $0.944$ on the validation subset and $0.955$ across all 60 sources. Three of the 60 sources still score below the gate on their own core-finding sentence. All entailment numbers in this paper come from the corrected instrument, and the pipeline aborts if the validation mean falls below $0.80$, a gate we set before rescoring. The corrected instrument leaves the preregistered log-rank test null in both regimes, which is the point, because a broken scorer and a real first-hop floor effect produce the same clean curve, and only the self-consistency check separates them.

A study that thresholds a model-derived score and compares groups across it can saturate the same way and produce a clean survival curve with a publishable null. The same check on the number extractor caught two faults, a confidence-interval hyphen read as a minus sign and a p-value written without its leading zero, each of which scored a preserved number as lost. With both corrected, the self-consistency check scores the corpus at $1.00$ instead of $0.88$, and every numeric figure here comes from the corrected extractor. The backward term falls for any summary, so forward entailment is the cleaner drift signal. Forward entailment itself falls from $0.97$ to $0.49$ under neutral prompting, against $0.97$ to $0.78$ under conservative.

A second scorer from a different family reproduces the main-arm result, since we rescored all 4{,}200 generations with \texttt{roberta-large-mnli}, and, across the 3{,}600 main-arm generations, it tracks the primary model at Pearson $r = 0.91$ for forward entailment, while the regime contrast that carries the claim moves little, from $0.294$ to $0.288$, and Appendix~\ref{app:crosscheck} gives the rest. Both are NLI cross-encoders, and the agreement therefore bounds sensitivity to the scorer without showing that either tracks what a reader would call a surviving finding.

\section{Discussion}

Repeated summarization makes scientific claims less precise across all three model families. This failure differs from the exaggeration reported for press releases, and it is harder to catch, because a vague claim carries no false statement for a fact-check to find.

The first hop does most of the numeric damage, and the intervention that matters is therefore local. Conservative prompting reduces numeric loss by $81\%$ with no training and no second model call, at $1.6$ times as many words.

\textbf{Limitations.} Our chains are homogeneous, in that one model summarizes its own output, while real pipelines mix models, humans, and formats. All three models are open weight at 3.8 to 7B parameters, and we can't say how frontier proprietary models behave, and hedge use is likely to track instruction-tuning style. Rule-based instruments measure surface markers of hedging, causal language, and content retention, and they can't see the beliefs behind those markers, and the entailment arm closes only part of that gap. The causal scale registers under half of deliberate strengthening rewrites, so we report its null as a bound on what that instrument detects rather than as evidence that overstatement is absent. Even where these markers track style more than belief, readers calibrate their trust on the style a summary presents. The depth distribution treats citation hops as summarization events, which no part of our data observes, and it comes from a branch-limited walk seeded by 60 abstracts, so the composition is a hypothetical application of measured trajectories to a plausible depth profile and the least certain quantitative claim in the paper. 

All 60 abstracts are biomedical, and an abstract isn't a full paper, and fields with other hedging conventions can show a different drift. Of the two H2 tests, the continuous one is exploratory, its p-values in the text are uncorrected, and it rests on the same 60 clusters that bound every estimate we report. 

Exact matching counts a rounded number as lost and a ratio beside its percentage equivalent counts twice, and both push the measure below true retention, while the generation side pulls the other way, because a source statistic counts as retained when a number of equal value appears anywhere in the summary. The net direction of the level is therefore unresolved, and no human has validated either NLI scorer. A summary that keeps a token doesn't always keep its meaning, and $12\%$ of generations that retain four fifths of their source statistics score below $0.5$ on forward entailment, rising to $28\%$ under neutral prompting (Appendix~\ref{app:meaning}).

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

Every number in this paper except the two pre-fix diagnostics named below is recomputable from the anonymized code and data archive submitted with this paper as supplementary material, where \texttt{src/paper\_numbers.py} regenerates them, and we will release that archive under a permissive license upon acceptance. We make no availability claim beyond the archive, and a reader who can't reach it should treat every number here as unreplicated. The archive contains the corpus with class labels, all 4{,}200 generations, the score tables from both scorers, the hypothesis outputs with the estimator recorded per test, the depth and composition outputs, the figures, and the preregistration. Chain generation takes 4.1 to 7.0 T4-hours per model, while every later stage runs in under an hour, and three files under \texttt{tests/} run the suite, which includes the self-entailment gate of Section~\ref{sec:validation}.

The $0.056$ self-entailment mean and the 287-token median premise come from the discarded pre-fix run and survive only in \texttt{release/FINDINGS.md}. The preregistration records two deviations, the estimator fallback and the Phi-3.5-mini substitution, both described in Section~\ref{sec:design}, and Appendix~\ref{app:practical} lists the implementation decisions that cost the most time.

\section{Conclusion}

The certainty we preregistered against doesn't appear in our data, and what a reader loses to a summarization chain is instead the numbers and qualifiers that made the claim checkable, most of them at the first hop. Our own first null came from a saturated instrument, and the harness of Section~\ref{sec:repro} lets anyone remeasure these rates as models change, which we would like to see done on frontier models and on chains that mix models with human editors.

\section{The second NLI scorer}
\label{app:crosscheck}

The entailment arm in Section~\ref{sec:validation} rests on one cross-encoder, and this pipeline has already produced one artifact from that same dependence, so we rescored every generation with a second model chosen to share as little as possible with the first. \texttt{roberta-large-mnli} is a 355M RoBERTa trained on MNLI alone, against the primary 184M DeBERTa-v3 trained on a multi-corpus NLI blend, and it runs through the same sentence-level aggregation and the same self-entailment gate.

It passes that gate at $0.989$, above the primary model's $0.955$, and across the 3{,}600 main-arm generations, its scores track the primary at Pearson $r = 0.91$ for forward entailment, $0.95$ for backward, $0.95$ for the bidirectional minimum, and $0.81$ for core-finding entailment, with Spearman coefficients within $0.02$ of the corresponding Pearson value. The second model reads higher on every one of those four quantities, by $4.2$ points on forward entailment where its mean is $0.70$ against $0.66$, and by $7.5$ points on core-finding entailment, so the two scorers differ in calibration and not in direction.

The paper's claim is the regime contrast, and it survives, because conservative prompting holds $0.294$ more forward entailment than neutral at generation ten under the primary model and $0.288$ under the second, and both scorers put the neutral endpoint near $0.5$ and the conservative endpoint near $0.8$. Two scorers agreeing doesn't establish that either measures entailment as a reader would judge it.

\section{What is confirmatory, what is exploratory, and what multiplicity costs}
\label{app:multiplicity}

The preregistration fixed five markers in two regimes for H1, a log-rank comparison in two regimes for H2, and five regime interactions for H3, which is seventeen confirmatory tests, and the manuscript Holm-corrects the five markers within each regime and the five interactions across markers. Everything else we report is exploratory and carries no correction in the text, which covers the continuous H2 regression in its full-chain and after-first-hop windows, the raw hedge count, the word-count control, the per-sentence normalization, the epistemic-hedge subset, three per-model estimates and four temperature-arm estimates, for thirteen tests in all.

Applying Holm across that whole exploratory family, nine of the thirteen still clear $0.05$. The four that don't are the after-first-hop null-result excess at $0.164$, the temperature-arm hedge density at $0.273$, the raw hedge count at $0.508$, and the temperature-arm causal strength at $0.858$, and the last two are results we report as null in any case. The continuous H2 excess over the full chain clears at $0.043$, so the finding that null-result abstracts lose their core finding faster survives a correction it was never given, while the claim that the gap persists after the first hop doesn't.

We keep this ledger separate from the confirmatory correction, because the two families answer different questions and pooling them would understate the preregistered results and overstate the others.

\section{Design checks against the release}
\label{app:design}

We checked three design claims against the score tables, and each one holds. The main arm holds 3{,}600 scored generations from 360 chains over 60 source abstracts, and each source contributes 60 generations and the effective sample for every interval is 60. The intraclass correlation across source abstracts is $0.27$ to $0.58$ on the levels of the four continuous markers and $0.005$ to $0.023$ on the adjacent-generation differences we model, which is why the random intercept had nothing left to absorb and the clustered fallback fired in every main-arm cell. All 4{,}200 rows of the cross-check table carry a forward-entailment value, split 3{,}600 main and 600 sensitivity, while the sensitivity table carries entailment columns with no populated values, which confirms that the primary scorer never ran on the temperature arm and that only the second scorer spans both arms.

\section{Practical considerations}
\label{app:practical}

Each of the following cost us at least a day, and none would be visible in the results had we not caught it.

\textbf{A class label named ``null''.} Our three classes are \texttt{rct}, \texttt{obs}, and \texttt{null}, and the score table stores the class as a string. \texttt{pandas} treats the string ``null'' as a missing value by default, and every grouped analysis therefore dropped the entire null-result class and reported two classes where there were three. We now pass \texttt{keep\_default\_na=False} with an explicit list of missing-value tokens when reading the table, and a regression test reloads it and asserts that all three class labels survive.

\textbf{Resuming interrupted chains.} A Kaggle session ends after twelve hours whether or not a model has finished its 120 chains. Our first resume logic restarted any incomplete chain from the source abstract, a behavior that regenerated the early generations under a different context and appended them as duplicate rows, so a resumed run carried two copies of generations one through $k$ for every chain it touched. Resume is now keyed on the last written generation and its text, so a chain continues from where it stopped, and the scorer also deduplicates on (abstract, model, regime, generation) and warns when it does.

\textbf{Child-process output on Kaggle.} The chain generator runs as a subprocess inside the notebook, and Kaggle drops long subprocess output, so a kernel in the middle of a healthy run showed an empty log for hours and looked hung. The notebook itself now reads the child's stdout line by line and prints each line with a flush.

\textbf{Notebook secrets.} A Hugging Face token stored at the account level isn't visible to a notebook unless the notebook attaches it, so the harness fell back to the ungated model (Section~\ref{sec:design}), and the harness now prints the swap when it makes one.

\textbf{Download backend.} Model downloads hung without progress on Kaggle with the default Hugging Face transfer backend, and setting \texttt{HF\_HUB\_DISABLE\_XET=1} before importing the library fixed it.

\textbf{Document-length premises.} Section~\ref{sec:validation} describes this failure, which the sentence-level scorer repairs, since it splits both premise and hypothesis into sentences, takes the maximum entailment over premise sentences for each hypothesis sentence, and averages over hypothesis sentences, so the 512-token limit is never reached and the self-entailment gate reads $0.944$ on its validation subset.

Figure~\ref{fig:depth} composes the per-generation rates along real citation depths, and the depth distribution comes from a forward-citation walk over OpenAlex~\citep{priem2022openalex}, seeded by all 60 source abstracts. The walk takes at most three citing works per node to a depth of four, orders reviews and meta-analyses first, and weights each reached work by its own citation count. \section{One chain, followed}
\label{app:case}

One chain shows the loss developing, where a null-result abstract on subclinical hypothyroidism and dyslipidemia in children reports five p-values and says the association ``still seems to be inconsistent based on the results of this and previous studies''. The first neutral summary keeps none of the five p-values, and core-finding entailment falls from $0.997$ to $0.010$ in that single hop, though the summary still carries the concession, ``given the inconsistency with previous findings'', that the question is open. By the tenth generation, the p-values are still absent, the concession is gone, and what remains is ``the study concluded that there is no correlation between subclinical hypothyroidism and dyslipidemia in children'', followed by a call for further research. That final sentence reproduces the source's own conclusion, which reads ``we found no correlation between SH and dyslipidemia in children'', so the tenth generation contains no claim that the abstract doesn't make. The models remove the evidence and the sentence that follows that conclusion, where the authors call the association inconsistent with previous studies, and almost all of the removal happens at the first hop. Dropping that qualification lets a conclusion the authors offered as provisional read as settled, which our markers don't score as overstatement because the causal scale sees the same relation type at both ends. No clinician has adjudicated this chain, and we report what changed and leave the judgment of whether the summary misleads a reader to human review. That concession is a clause, and no word in it appears on the hedge list, so its loss doesn't register in hedge density.

\section{The length band the two regimes share}
\label{app:length}

Conservative summaries average 209 words by whitespace count against 130 for neutral ones over generations one through ten, so the two regimes overlap only in part of their length range. The $195$ and $123$ of Section~\ref{sec:h3} count the same texts with the alphabetic word counter that normalizes hedge density, which drops numerals and bare symbols, so the two conventions differ by about $7\%$ on identical summaries and neither figure contradicts the other. We take the band running from the higher of the two fifth percentiles to the lower of the two ninety-fifth percentiles, which spans 137 to 222 words and holds 678 neutral and 1{,}064 conservative generations out of 3{,}600. Inside that band we average each instrument within source abstract and regime, difference the two regimes within abstract, and read the mean and its standard error across abstracts, which keeps the pairing that the design provides and clusters at the level the rest of the paper clusters at.

The numeric-fidelity gap falls from $+0.416$ over all lengths to $+0.231$ inside the band, with a $95\%$ interval of $0.143$ to $0.318$ over 45 abstracts. Qualifier retention shrinks the same way, from $+0.321$ to $+0.168$ on 50 abstracts, and its interval runs from $0.092$ to $0.243$. The forward-entailment gap is the smallest of the three inside the band, $+0.078$ against $+0.269$ over all lengths, and its interval of $0.027$ to $0.130$ over 53 abstracts still excludes zero. The retention instruments therefore keep about half of their advantage and the entailment instrument keeps under a third, and none of the three crosses zero.

Restricting to shared lengths isn't the same as matching them, because the summaries inside the band were still produced under different instructions and the models chose those lengths with no budget imposed. The comparison bounds how much of the H3 effect could be length alone, and it can't license a causal claim about instructions net of length. A design that would separate them gives both regimes an explicit output budget, measures realized length, and adds an extractive compression baseline at the same budget, and we haven't run it.

\section{Where exact numbers and preserved meaning part company}
\label{app:meaning}

Numeric fidelity counts a source statistic as kept when its token reappears, which is silent about whether the number still attaches to the same population, comparison, unit and finding. Among the 3{,}120 generations whose source carried at least one extractable statistic, 1{,}806 keep the exact form of at least four fifths of them, and 211 of those, or $11.7\%$, score below $0.5$ on forward entailment against the source. The share is $28.0\%$ of such generations under neutral prompting and $5.9\%$ under conservative prompting. Across all generations with a source statistic, numeric fidelity and forward entailment correlate at $r = 0.51$, which is far from the near-identity that would let one instrument stand in for the other.

Neither instrument settles what a reader would judge, so this figure bounds how often the two instruments disagree and counts no meaning errors. We release a 30-row sample of the discordant generations, stratified by class, model and regime, as the starting point for the blinded human adjudication that this construct needs and that we haven't performed.
