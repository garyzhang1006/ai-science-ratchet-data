\begin{abstract}
Scientific claims increasingly reach their readers through language-model intermediaries, and prior work shows that iterative LLM processing degrades information in general text. Whether that degradation has a direction on scientific claims is unstudied, and the difference matters, because unbiased noise averages out across a literature while systematic drift compounds. We preregistered the hypothesis that summarization chains push claims toward certainty, with fewer hedges and harder causal language, and measured it on 360 chains of depth ten built from 60 stratified PubMed abstracts across three open-weight models and two prompt regimes. The data reject the certainty hypothesis. Hedge density rises by $0.061$ per 100 words per generation ($p = 7 \times 10^{-8}$), the opposite of the prediction, and the reversal replicates in all three models with no sign flips; causal-claim strength does not move ($p = 0.70$). Exact retention of effect sizes, intervals, and p-values falls $0.046$ per generation and scope-qualifier retention falls $0.049$ per generation, both at $p < 10^{-39}$. The chains erode precision, so claims drift toward being unfalsifiable instead of overstated. The loss is also front-loaded. A single summarization destroys $41\%$ of a paper's reported numbers, which is $79\%$ of the numeric loss incurred over ten generations, so chain depth matters far less than whether any unassisted summarization happens. Prompting that demands preservation of every caveat cuts the numeric loss rate by $80\%$ at no cost in length. We release all chains, code, instruments, and measurements.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Between a scientific finding and the person who acts on it there is now, with growing frequency, a language model, and often several in sequence: one model summarizes the paper for a review, and another compresses the review into the paragraph a decision-maker reads. Each hop looks tolerable on its own, and iterative-generation experiments have begun to quantify the loss, showing that facts mutate and information degrades over repeated LLM processing of news and narrative text \citep{mohamed2025telephone}. That line of work has not asked whether the loss on scientific claims has a direction. We ask whether repeated AI summarization strips hedges, hardens causal language, and erases null results faster than symmetric noise would.

Noise and bias have different consequences. If each summarization step perturbs a claim symmetrically, aggregate scientific communication survives, because errors decorrelate across chains and readers and the literature's redundancy absorbs them. If each step instead pushes in one direction, toward the confidence and positivity that brevity favors, then every hop tightens a ratchet and small per-step biases become large end-to-end distortions at depths that scholarly communication already reaches. Psycholinguistics gives reasons to expect the directional case, since human serial reproduction conventionalizes and sharpens material toward schema-congruent forms \citep{bartlett1932remembering}, and instruction-tuned models are trained on human preferences that reward clear, confident prose. We preregistered that expectation.

The expectation failed. Hedges grow denser per hundred words under repeated summarization, across all three models we tested, and causal language holds still. Precision collapses instead: numbers and scope conditions fall away generation after generation while the hedges survive, so a claim that began as a bounded quantitative statement about a specific population ends as a hedged directional gesture that no reader can check. A certainty ratchet describes the opposite of what these pipelines do.

The shape of the loss also departs from the ratchet metaphor. Degradation is front-loaded: the first summarization destroys $41\%$ of the numeric content of an abstract, which is $79\%$ of all numeric loss over ten generations, and the curve flattens after that. Provenance requirements that shorten citation chains therefore buy much less than compounding would predict, because most of the damage is done at hop one, and the question that matters is whether a reader sits downstream of any unassisted summarization.

We measure all of this with automated, reproducible instruments, which avoids the single-annotator ceiling that limits much integrity research. Our contributions are the first measurement of the \emph{direction} of LLM summarization drift on scientific claims, with preregistered hypotheses and automated scoring; the finding that the direction is toward vagueness, replicated across three model families; the finding that loss is front-loaded, which redirects where interventions should bind; a released corpus of 4{,}200 scored generations; and a documented instrument-validation failure, caught by a self-entailment check, that would otherwise have produced a clean and spurious null.

\section{Related work}

Iterative generation distorts text in measurable ways. The broken-telephone line of work ran chains of translation and paraphrase through open-weight models and documented cumulative factual drift, its dependence on language and chain design, and its partial mitigation by conservative decoding \citep{mohamed2025telephone}. These studies quantify how much information moves; none scores the epistemic direction of the movement on scientific material, which is our object. Serial reproduction in humans, from Bartlett's original experiments onward \citep{bartlett1932remembering}, shows systematic conventionalization and sharpening, and our design is the LLM analogue of that tradition with instruments in place of annotators. Our result contrasts with it, since human serial reproduction sharpens and model serial reproduction blurs.

On the science-communication side, \citet{sumner2014exaggeration} found that exaggerated causal claims in health news track exaggeration already present in the academic press release, a human baseline our pipeline might have been expected to automate. Hedging and metadiscourse have quantitative traditions in applied linguistics \citep{hyland2005metadiscourse}, which we use directly. Prevalence work shows that LLMs already touch a material share of scientific text \citep{liang2024mapping,kobak2024delving}, which gives a per-step bias its population relevance, since a bias matters in proportion to how much text passes through it.

\section{Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Source claims and stratification}

We draw 60 abstracts from the PubMed open-access subset, stratified equally into three claim classes: randomized trials whose abstracts report at least one numeric effect size with an interval, observational studies with unnegated association language, and explicit null results, operationalized as a null phrase (``no significant difference'', ``did not differ'', ``was not associated'', and similar) appearing in the final third of the abstract. Assignment is mutually exclusive with null-priority, so an abstract whose conclusion reports a null result is classed null regardless of which query surfaced it; without that rule, fetch order silently absorbs null-result trials into the trial stratum. We keep abstracts of 150--450 words.

\subsection{Chains and regimes}

Each abstract seeds chains of depth ten under every combination of three models (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Phi-3.5-mini-instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3) and two prompt regimes: \emph{neutral}, which asks for a faithful summary at roughly the original length, and \emph{conservative}, which additionally instructs the model to preserve every hedge, qualifier, number, and statement of uncertainty. This gives 360 chains and 3{,}600 scored generations, all of which completed without truncation. Decoding is greedy, to separate systematic drift from sampling variance; a sensitivity arm reruns a stratified 20-abstract subset through all three models at temperature $0.7$ with a fixed seed, adding 600 further generations.

\subsection{Instruments}

Five instruments score every generation, all deterministic given released code. Hedge density counts matches against the hedge sublist of Hyland's metadiscourse lexicon \citep{hyland2005metadiscourse}, normalized per 100 words. Causal strength grades the strongest claim sentence on a five-point scale from no-relation to unqualified causation, with negation scoped to clause boundaries so that a safety clause such as ``there were no dropouts'' cannot invert the efficacy claim that follows it. Numeric fidelity extracts effect sizes, confidence intervals, p-values, and percentages by pattern and scores exact retention against the source, excluding the confidence level itself so that preserving the boilerplate ``95\% CI'' earns no credit. Qualifier retention tracks population, dosage, and study-design phrases by token overlap. Entailment scores each generation against the source bidirectionally with a public NLI cross-encoder.

The entailment instrument required a correction that we report because it nearly produced a false result, described in Section~\ref{sec:validation}.

\subsection{Analysis}

The estimand for H1 is the mean adjacent-generation change in each marker. We specified a mixed model with a random intercept per source abstract, with a cluster-robust OLS fallback; the fallback fired in every cell, because differencing removes the abstract-level intercept by construction and leaves no level variance for a random intercept to absorb. All reported standard errors are therefore clustered on the source abstract, which carries the dependence among the nine differences within a chain. We Holm-correct across the five markers within each regime. H2 compares erosion of the core finding across claim classes, and H3 interacts regime with step. We fixed all hypotheses, model specifications, and exclusion rules in a time-stamped preregistration in the repository.

\subsection{Drift runs toward vagueness}

Table~\ref{tab:h1} gives the per-step effects. Hedge density rises by $0.061$ per 100 words per generation under neutral prompting ($p = 6.9 \times 10^{-8}$), the opposite of the preregistered direction, and causal-claim strength does not move ($+0.0067$, $p = 0.70$). Over ten generations, mean hedge density climbs from $0.72$ to $1.33$ per 100 words, an $85\%$ increase. The two markers that were supposed to carry the certainty ratchet run backwards or stay flat.

The rise is in proportion. The raw number of hedges per summary is flat to slightly declining, at $-0.018$ per generation ($p = 0.25$), while neutral summaries shrink from 210 words to 116, so the hedges that survive occupy a larger share of a smaller text because the models shed numbers and qualifiers faster than hedges. Compression alone does not explain the rise: regressing the per-step change in density on the per-step change in word count leaves a residual density increase of $+0.045$ per generation ($p < 10^{-3}$). The proportion is also the quantity a reader experiences, since the text arrives as delivered, with progressively more hedge and less substance.

Hedge density rises in each model separately, with no sign flips on any marker: Qwen2.5-7B $+0.0499$ ($p = 4.0 \times 10^{-4}$), Phi-3.5-mini $+0.0969$ ($p = 3.1 \times 10^{-6}$), Mistral-7B $+0.0366$ ($p = 2.0 \times 10^{-4}$).

The precision of the claim degrades instead. Exact retention of effect sizes, intervals, and p-values falls $0.046$ per generation, scope-qualifier retention falls $0.049$ per generation, and bidirectional entailment against the source falls $0.068$ per generation, all at $p < 10^{-39}$. Pooled over the neutral regime, numeric fidelity drops from $0.88$ to $0.42$ and qualifier retention from $0.98$ to $0.49$ across ten generations. A claim thus arrives at the end of a chain with roughly half its numbers, half its scope conditions, and nearly twice its original hedge density, which is vagueness: the endpoint is a statement too imprecise to check, and it asserts no more than the evidence supports.

\subsection{The first hop does most of the damage}

Figure~\ref{fig:drift} shows a shape the ratchet framing did not anticipate: the first summarization does most of the damage. Numeric fidelity falls from $0.88$ to $0.52$ between the source and generation one, a loss of $41\%$ of the original content that accounts for $79\%$ of all numeric loss across the full ten generations, and the first hop accounts for $52\%$ of total qualifier loss. After the first hop the curves flatten, so whatever survives one summarization tends to survive nine more.

For anyone designing interventions this shape matters more than the direction result. Under exponential compounding, shortening chains would be the lever and a two-hop pipeline would be far safer than a four-hop one. In the measured data the dominant risk sits at the transition from the source document to any machine summary of it, and further hops add little. Provenance requirements that reduce chain depth therefore buy far less than the compounding model predicts, while a policy that keeps a reader one hop from the source, or that preserves the source's numbers through the first hop, captures most of the available benefit.

\subsection{Conservative prompting damps every rate at no cost in length}

Instructing the model to preserve every hedge, qualifier, number, and statement of uncertainty reduces the drift magnitude of every marker without flipping any sign, which is what H3 predicted. Numeric loss falls by $79.7\%$ ($p = 9.8 \times 10^{-47}$ on the interaction), qualifier loss by $76.9\%$ ($p = 1.6 \times 10^{-27}$), entailment loss by $58.9\%$, and the hedge-density rise by $45.7\%$ ($p = 0.012$). At generation ten the conservative regime retains numeric fidelity of $0.79$ against $0.42$ under neutral prompting, and qualifier retention of $0.86$ against $0.49$.

The intervention is close to free. Conservative summaries average 195 words against 123 for neutral ones, from a 210-word source, so the regime that preserves the science also stays closer to the original length. Every rate remains significantly nonzero, so prompting leaves the problem in place, but it is a large effect available at no computational cost.

\subsection{Null results erode fastest under the continuous test}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig2_survival.pdf}
\caption{Survival of the core finding under repeated summarization, by claim class and regime, with the erosion event defined as core-finding entailment falling below $0.5$. The neutral regime saturates at the first generation, which is why the preregistered log-rank comparison across classes has little room to discriminate.}
\label{fig:halflife}
\end{figure}

The preregistered H2 test, the first generation at which core-finding entailment drops below $0.5$ compared across claim classes by log-rank, is null in both regimes (neutral $p = 0.40$, conservative $p = 0.21$). Figure~\ref{fig:halflife} shows why it has little power: under neutral prompting the median half-life is a single generation for every class, so nearly every chain crosses the binary threshold at the first hop.

The continuous form of the same question does discriminate. Regressing core-finding entailment on generation interacted with a null-result indicator, over all eleven generations of the neutral regime with errors clustered on abstract, gives a baseline decay of $-0.032$ per generation ($p < 10^{-14}$) and an additional $-0.017$ per generation for null-result abstracts ($p = 0.009$). Null findings lose their core claim about $1.5$ times as fast as positive ones, the direction H2 predicted, and the gap persists after the first hop: restricted to generations one through ten, the baseline decay is $-0.0078$ ($p = 0.048$) and the null excess $-0.0145$ ($p = 0.041$). Class trajectories bear this out: null-result core findings fall from $0.97$ to $0.21$ over ten generations, against $0.37$ for observational and $0.32$ for trial abstracts. We report this as secondary evidence, because it is an uncorrected test that we did not preregister, it supplements a preregistered test that came out null, and it deserves replication before it carries weight.

One chain shows the shape of the loss. A null-result abstract on subclinical hypothyroidism and dyslipidemia in children reports five p-values and closes by saying the association ``still seems to be inconsistent based on the results of this and previous studies''. The first neutral summary keeps none of the five p-values, and core-finding entailment falls from $0.997$ to $0.010$ in that single hop, though the summary still concedes, ``given the inconsistency with previous findings'', that the question is open. By the tenth generation the p-values are still absent, the concession is gone, and what remains is ``the study concluded that there is no correlation between subclinical hypothyroidism and dyslipidemia in children'' followed by a generic call for further research. The chain never overstated the claim in the usual direction, since it stays a null. It removed, almost all at the first hop, the evidence and the acknowledgment that the evidence conflicts, which is what a reader needs in order to weigh a null result.

\subsection{Temperature sensitivity}

Rerunning a stratified 20-abstract subset through all three models at temperature $0.7$ reproduces every sign and nearly every magnitude: hedge density $+0.0515$ against $+0.0527$ for greedy decoding on the same abstracts, numeric fidelity $-0.0511$ against $-0.0399$, qualifier retention $-0.0546$ against $-0.0443$, and causal strength null in both. Information loss is slightly steeper under sampling. Hedge density keeps its effect size and loses significance ($p = 0.09$) on the smaller subset, a power difference with the estimate unchanged. Greedy decoding does not produce the drift.

\subsection{From per-step rates to consumed claims}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig3_composition.pdf}
\caption{Composing measured drift along real intermediation depths. Left: consumption-weighted depth distribution estimated from OpenAlex forward-citation paths over 30 seed works. Right: expected retention at depth under measured neutral-regime rates, with the $0.5$ calibration floor marked.}
\label{fig:depth}
\end{figure}

A forward-citation walk over OpenAlex, starting from our seed abstracts and weighting each reached work by its own citation count, yields 2{,}327 consumption-weighted samples and puts the median intermediation depth of a consumed claim at two hops, with a 90th percentile of four. Composing the measured neutral-regime rates along this distribution, a claim at the median depth of two hops retains $87\%$ of its original qualifiers, the expectation over the whole depth distribution is $86\%$, and qualifier retention stays above a $0.5$ calibration floor out to ten hops. Hedge density rises with depth, so its per-step ratio exceeds one and no depth threshold bounds it from above.

These composed figures are milder than the raw trajectories because of the front-loading result. The first hop dominates, so the marginal cost of the second and third hops that typify real consumption is small, and the danger lies in whether the text a reader receives passed through any unassisted summarization, whatever its depth in the citation graph. Two caveats bound this analysis: it treats a citation hop as a summarization event, which is an approximation, and it rests on 30 seed works.

\section{Instrument validation and a spurious null}
\label{sec:validation}

Our first entailment run produced a clean H2 result, a median half-life of exactly one generation in every claim class in both regimes, and it was an artifact.

A simple check caught it, since a text must entail a sentence copied out of itself. Scoring each source abstract against its own core-finding sentence returned a mean entailment of $0.056$, when it should sit near one, so the instrument was reporting almost nothing as supported, every chain crossed the erosion threshold immediately, and the classes became indistinguishable by construction. Truncation does not explain it, since the median premise is 287 tokens against a 512-token limit; the cause was the model itself, because a sentence-pair NLI cross-encoder is trained on single-sentence premises and mislabels document-length ones.

Replacing whole-document scoring with granular aggregation in the style of SummaC \citep{laban2022summac}, comparing each hypothesis sentence against every premise sentence and keeping the best-supporting one, raised self-entailment to $0.944$ using the same model. All entailment numbers in this paper come from the corrected instrument, and the scoring pipeline now aborts if self-entailment falls below $0.80$.

We report this at length because the failure mode generalizes. A saturating instrument still produces well-formed survival curves and a publishable null, so any study that thresholds a model-derived score and compares groups can fail this way, and a self-consistency check costs one line and catches it. Backward entailment falls for any summary, because summaries drop content by design, so it measures compression as much as infidelity; forward entailment is the cleaner drift signal, and it falls from $0.97$ to $0.49$ under neutral prompting against $0.97$ to $0.78$ under conservative.

\section{Discussion}

Repeated summarization blurs scientific claims. Across three model families, ten generations, and two prompting regimes, it strips the numbers and the scope conditions that make a claim checkable while sparing the hedges, so the surviving text reads as more tentative, and it leaves causal language untouched. A finding that began as a bounded quantitative statement about a specified population arrives as a hedged directional gesture. This failure differs from the exaggeration documented for press releases, and in one respect it is worse, because a reader who checks an overstated claim against the source will notice the discrepancy, while a vague claim gives the check nothing to grip.

The front-loading result redirects where effort should go. If loss compounded with depth, the intervention would be structural, shortening the chains between primary literature and readers. Because $79\%$ of numeric loss occurs at the first hop, the intervention is local: preserve the numbers and qualifiers through the transition from source document to first machine summary. Conservative prompting, which cuts numeric loss by $80\%$ at no cost in length, is one cheap version of that intervention, and the fact that a prompt achieves it suggests the models have the capacity to keep the information and drop it by default.

Beyond the specific numbers, the study argues for a method. Epistemic properties of AI-mediated communication are measurable with instruments anyone can run, and system-level consequences follow from laboratory rates by composition along measured usage structure, so debates about what AI does to science can proceed on quantities. The same method requires validating instruments before trusting them, as Section~\ref{sec:validation} shows, and letting a preregistered hypothesis fail in public, as ours did.

\section{Reproducibility}

Everything needed to recompute every number in this paper is public at \url{https://github.com/garyzhang1006/ai-science-ratchet-data}. The release contains the 60-abstract corpus with stratum labels and the 20-abstract sensitivity subset; all 4{,}200 generations as JSONL, one file per model; the full per-generation score table; the hypothesis outputs with the estimator recorded per test; a script, \texttt{src/paper\_numbers.py}, that recomputes every figure quoted in the running text from the released scores and chains; the OpenAlex depth distribution and the composition output; the three figures and the results table; and the time-stamped preregistration with its deviations section, which records both the estimator fallback and a model substitution. Chain generation ran on a single free-tier T4 in roughly five hours per model; every other stage runs on a laptop CPU in minutes. The instrument test suite, including the self-entailment gate described in Section~\ref{sec:validation}, runs with \texttt{python tests/test\_instruments.py} and \texttt{python tests/test\_regressions.py}.

The preregistration records two deviations. All H1 estimates come from the pre-specified cluster-robust OLS fallback rather than the mixed model, for the structural reason given in Section~\ref{sec:design}. The third model was specified as Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and executed as Phi-3.5-mini-instruct, because the gated-model token was unavailable in the compute environment and the harness substitutes an ungated fallback automatically; the released model set is therefore ungated, which simplifies replication.

\section{Limitations}

Our chains are homogeneous, one model talking to itself, while real pipelines mix models, humans, and formats; the released harness accepts arbitrary chain compositions, and mixed chains are the natural next experiment. The three models are open-weight and 3.8--7B parameters, so behavior of frontier proprietary models is untested and could differ, particularly on hedging, which plausibly tracks instruction-tuning style. Rule-based instruments measure surface markers of epistemic stance rather than beliefs, and although the entailment arm guards the construct, a determined skeptic can read our effects as stylistic; we reply that consumed style is what downstream readers calibrate on. The depth distribution treats citation hops as summarization events and rests on 30 seed works, which makes the composition analysis the softest quantitative claim in the paper. Abstracts are not full papers, and drift measured on abstracts may differ from drift on sections. The secondary H2 result is a single uncorrected test. Models also improve, so the measured rates are a 2026 snapshot and the harness will outlast the numbers.

\section{Conclusion}

We asked whether AI summarization of science drifts with a direction, preregistered the answer that it drifts toward certainty, and found the opposite. Hedge density rises, causal language holds still, and precision collapses, so the claims that reach readers through machine intermediaries are vaguer than the science they came from. The damage is concentrated in the first summarization, which makes the first hop the intervention point, and a prompt that asks for caveats to be preserved recovers most of what is otherwise lost. We release all chains, code, instruments, preregistration, and measurements so that the rates can be recomputed as the models change.
