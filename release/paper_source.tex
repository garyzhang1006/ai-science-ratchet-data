\title{The Vagueness Ratchet: Repeated AI Summarization Makes\\Scientific Claims Less Precise, Not More Confident}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Scientific claims now reach many of their readers through language models, and iterative LLM processing is known to degrade information in general text. Whether that degradation carries a direction on scientific claims has not been asked, and the answer matters, because unbiased noise averages out across a literature while a systematic drift compounds. We preregistered the prediction that summarization chains push claims toward certainty, with fewer hedges and harder causal language, then measured it on 360 chains of depth ten built from 60 stratified PubMed abstracts, three open-weight models, and two prompt regimes. Hedge density instead rises by $0.061$ per 100 words per generation ($p = 7 \times 10^{-8}$), against the preregistered direction, and the reversal holds in all three models with no sign flips, while causal-claim strength does not move ($p = 0.70$). What falls is precision. Exact retention of effect sizes, intervals, and p-values drops $0.046$ per generation and scope-qualifier retention drops $0.049$ per generation, both at $p < 10^{-39}$, so claims end up unfalsifiable rather than overstated. The loss also arrives early. One summarization destroys $41\%$ of a paper's reported numbers, which is $79\%$ of everything lost over ten generations, so the depth of a citation chain matters much less than whether any unassisted summarization happened at all. A prompt that demands every caveat be preserved cuts the numeric loss rate by $80\%$ and costs nothing in length. We release the chains, code, instruments, and measurements.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A scientific finding now often reaches the person who acts on it through a language model, and sometimes through several. One model summarizes the paper for a review; another compresses the review into the paragraph a decision-maker actually reads. \citet{mohamed2025telephone} measured what this costs on news and narrative text, where facts mutate and information degrades under repeated processing. They did not ask whether the loss on scientific claims runs in a particular direction, and that is our question: does repeated AI summarization strip hedges, harden causal language, and erase null results faster than symmetric noise would?

The distinction matters because noise and bias behave differently in aggregate. Symmetric perturbation leaves scientific communication roughly intact, since errors decorrelate across chains and readers and the redundancy of a literature absorbs them. A consistent push in one direction behaves like a ratchet, where each hop holds what the last one took and per-step biases that look negligible compound into end-to-end distortion at depths scholarly communication already reaches. We had reasons to expect the second case. \citet{bartlett1932remembering} showed that human serial reproduction conventionalizes and sharpens material toward schema-congruent forms, and instruction-tuned models learn from human preferences that reward clear, confident prose. So we preregistered it.

That prediction did not survive the data. Hedges grow denser per hundred words under repeated summarization in all three models we tested, and causal language holds still. Precision is what collapses. Numbers and scope conditions fall away generation after generation while the hedges survive, so an abstract that opened with a bounded quantitative statement about a specific population arrives ten hops later as a hedged directional gesture with nothing left in it for a reader to check. Whatever these pipelines do to a claim, it is not a certainty ratchet.

The shape of the loss also came out wrong. Degradation concentrates at the front, where the first summarization destroys $41\%$ of the numeric content of an abstract and accounts for $79\%$ of all numeric loss over ten generations, after which the curve flattens. This inverts the obvious policy response. Provenance rules that shorten citation chains buy much less than a compounding model predicts, because the damage is already done at hop one, and a reader is better served by knowing whether the text in front of them passed through any unassisted summarization at all.

Our instruments are automated and deterministic, which sidesteps the single-annotator ceiling that limits much integrity research and lets anyone rerun them. Concretely, we contribute (1) the first measurement of the \emph{direction} of LLM summarization drift on scientific claims, with preregistered hypotheses and automated scoring, (2) the finding that the direction is toward vagueness, replicated across three model families, (3) the finding that loss is front-loaded, which changes where an intervention should bind, (4) a released corpus of 4{,}200 scored generations, and (5) a documented instrument failure, caught by a self-entailment check, that would otherwise have produced a clean and entirely spurious null.

\section{Related work}

Iterative generation distorts text in measurable ways. \citet{mohamed2025telephone} ran chains of translation and paraphrase through open-weight models and documented cumulative factual drift, its dependence on language and chain design, and its partial mitigation by conservative decoding. Their work quantifies how much information moves; it does not score the epistemic direction of the movement on scientific material, which is our object. Serial reproduction in humans, from Bartlett's original experiments onward \citep{bartlett1932remembering}, shows systematic conventionalization and sharpening, and our design is the LLM analogue of that tradition with instruments in place of annotators. Our result runs the other way. Human serial reproduction sharpens; model serial reproduction blurs.

On the science-communication side, \citet{sumner2014exaggeration} found that exaggerated causal claims in health news track exaggeration already present in the academic press release, a human baseline our pipeline might have been expected to automate. Hedging and metadiscourse have quantitative traditions in applied linguistics, and we take our hedge list straight from \citet{hyland2005metadiscourse}. \citet{liang2024mapping} and \citet{kobak2024delving} estimated that LLMs already touch a material share of scientific text, which gives a per-step bias its population relevance, since a bias matters in proportion to how much text passes through it.

\section{Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Source claims and stratification}

We draw 60 abstracts from the PubMed open-access subset, stratified equally into three claim classes: randomized trials whose abstracts report at least one numeric effect size with an interval, observational studies with unnegated association language, and explicit null results, operationalized as a null phrase (``no significant difference'', ``did not differ'', ``was not associated'', and similar) appearing in the final third of the abstract. Assignment is mutually exclusive with null-priority, so an abstract whose conclusion reports a null result is classed null regardless of which query surfaced it; without that rule, fetch order silently absorbs null-result trials into the trial stratum. We keep abstracts of 150--450 words.

\subsection{Chains and regimes}

Each abstract seeds chains of depth ten under every combination of three models (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Phi-3.5-mini-instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3) and two prompt regimes: \emph{neutral}, which asks for a faithful summary at roughly the original length, and \emph{conservative}, which additionally instructs the model to preserve every hedge, qualifier, number, and statement of uncertainty. This gives 360 chains and 3{,}600 scored generations, all of which completed without truncation. Decoding is greedy, to separate systematic drift from sampling variance; a sensitivity arm reruns a stratified 20-abstract subset through all three models at temperature $0.7$ with a fixed seed, adding 600 further generations. We ran every chain on a single Kaggle T4 with 4-bit NF4 quantization, at roughly five hours per model.\footnote{We preregistered Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct as the third model. The Hugging Face token for that gated model never reached the Kaggle kernel, because an account-level secret is not attached to a notebook unless the notebook opts in, so the harness fell back to Phi-3.5-mini-instruct as its fallback table specifies. We noticed only after the Phi run had finished and kept the ungated set rather than spend another five T4-hours, which also means anyone can replicate the released model set without a gated-model token.}

\subsection{Instruments}

Five instruments score every generation, all deterministic given released code. Hedge density counts matches against a 101-item hedge list drawn from the hedges category of Hyland's metadiscourse appendix \citep{hyland2005metadiscourse}, normalized per 100 words.\footnote{Word counts throughout use the hedge instrument's tokenizer, which ignores pure numerals and punctuation tokens, so they run a little under a whitespace count.} Causal strength grades the strongest claim sentence on a five-point scale from no-relation to unqualified causation, with negation scoped to clause boundaries so that a safety clause such as ``there were no dropouts'' can't invert the efficacy claim that follows it. Numeric fidelity extracts effect sizes, confidence intervals, p-values, and percentages by pattern and scores exact retention against the source, excluding the confidence level itself so that preserving the boilerplate ``95\% CI'' earns no credit. Qualifier retention tracks population, dosage, and study-design phrases by token overlap. Entailment scores each generation against the source bidirectionally with \texttt{cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-base}, a public sentence-pair cross-encoder. That last instrument nearly produced a false result, and Section~\ref{sec:validation} reports what went wrong.

\subsection{Analysis}

The estimand for H1 is the mean adjacent-generation change in each marker. We specified a mixed model with a random intercept per source abstract and a cluster-robust OLS fallback. The fallback fired in every cell. Differencing removes the abstract-level intercept by construction and leaves no level variance for a random intercept to absorb, so all reported standard errors are clustered on the source abstract, which carries the dependence among the nine differences within a chain; there are 1{,}800 such differences per regime for most markers, 1{,}560 for numeric fidelity, since abstracts with no extractable statistic drop out, and 1{,}710 for qualifier retention. We Holm-correct across the five markers within each regime. Our three preregistered hypotheses are (1) H1, that every marker drifts toward certainty, (2) H2, that the core finding of a null-result abstract erodes faster than that of a positive one, and (3) H3, that conservative prompting damps every rate without flipping a sign. We fixed all hypotheses, model specifications, and exclusion rules in a time-stamped preregistration in the repository.

\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Drift runs toward vagueness}

Table~\ref{tab:h1} gives the per-step effects. Hedge density rises by $0.061$ per 100 words per generation under neutral prompting ($p = 6.9 \times 10^{-8}$), the opposite of the preregistered direction, and causal-claim strength does not move ($+0.0067$, $p = 0.70$). Over ten generations, mean hedge density climbs from $0.72$ to $1.33$ per 100 words, an $85\%$ increase. Neither marker behaves as a certainty ratchet requires.

The rise is in proportion. The raw number of hedges per summary is flat to slightly declining, at $-0.018$ per generation ($p = 0.25$), while neutral summaries shrink from 210 words to 116, so the hedges that survive occupy a larger share of a smaller text because the models shed numbers and qualifiers faster than hedges. We checked whether compression alone explains the rise: regressing the per-step change in density on the per-step change in word count leaves a residual density increase of $+0.045$ per generation ($p < 10^{-3}$), so it doesn't. The proportion is also the quantity a reader experiences, since the text arrives as delivered, with progressively more hedge and less substance.

Hedge density rises in each model separately, with no sign flips on any marker: Qwen2.5-7B $+0.0499$ ($p = 4.0 \times 10^{-4}$), Phi-3.5-mini $+0.0969$ ($p = 3.1 \times 10^{-6}$), Mistral-7B $+0.0366$ ($p = 2.0 \times 10^{-4}$). Phi, the smallest of the three, shows the largest rise.

The precision of the claim degrades instead. Exact retention of effect sizes, intervals, and p-values falls $0.046$ per generation, scope-qualifier retention falls $0.049$ per generation, and bidirectional entailment against the source falls $0.068$ per generation, all at $p < 10^{-39}$. Pooled over the neutral regime, numeric fidelity drops from $0.88$ to $0.42$ and qualifier retention from $0.98$ to $0.49$ across ten generations. A claim thus arrives at the end of a chain with roughly half its numbers, half its scope conditions, and nearly twice its original hedge density, which is vagueness: the endpoint is a statement too imprecise to check, and it asserts no more than the evidence supports.

\subsection{The first hop does most of the damage}

Figure~\ref{fig:drift} shows a shape the ratchet framing did not anticipate. Numeric fidelity falls from $0.88$ to $0.52$ between the source and generation one, a loss of $41\%$ of the original content that accounts for $79\%$ of all numeric loss across the full ten generations, and the first hop accounts for $52\%$ of total qualifier loss. The curves then flatten. Whatever survives one summarization tends to survive nine more.

For anyone designing interventions this shape matters more than the direction result. Under exponential compounding, shortening chains would be the lever and a two-hop pipeline would be far safer than a four-hop one. In the measured data the dominant risk sits at the transition from the source document to any machine summary of it, and further hops add little: if you cut a pipeline from four hops to two, you recover little, because most of the numbers were gone after hop one. Provenance requirements that reduce chain depth therefore buy far less than the compounding model predicts, while a policy that keeps a reader one hop from the source, or that preserves the source's numbers through the first hop, captures most of the available benefit.

\subsection{Conservative prompting damps every rate at no cost in length}

Instructing the model to preserve every hedge, qualifier, number, and statement of uncertainty reduces the drift magnitude of every marker without flipping any sign, which is what H3 predicted, and it does so handily. Numeric loss falls by $79.7\%$ ($p = 9.8 \times 10^{-47}$ on the interaction), qualifier loss by $76.9\%$ ($p = 1.6 \times 10^{-27}$), entailment loss by $58.9\%$, and the hedge-density rise by $45.7\%$ ($p = 0.012$). At generation ten the conservative regime retains numeric fidelity of $0.79$ against $0.42$ under neutral prompting, and qualifier retention of $0.86$ against $0.49$.

The intervention is close to free. Conservative summaries average 195 words against 123 for neutral ones, from a 210-word source, so the regime that preserves the science also stays closer to the original length. Every rate remains significantly nonzero, so prompting leaves the problem in place, but it is a large effect available at no computational cost, a cheap trick that any deployment can adopt today.

\subsection{Null results erode fastest under the continuous test}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig2_survival.pdf}
\caption{Survival of the core finding under repeated summarization, by claim class and regime, with the erosion event defined as core-finding entailment falling below $0.5$. The neutral regime saturates at the first generation, which is why the preregistered log-rank comparison across classes has little room to discriminate.}
\label{fig:halflife}
\end{figure}

The preregistered H2 test, the first generation at which core-finding entailment drops below $0.5$ compared across claim classes by log-rank, is null in both regimes (neutral $p = 0.40$, conservative $p = 0.21$). The binary test saturates. Figure~\ref{fig:halflife} shows why: under neutral prompting the median half-life is a single generation for every class, so nearly every chain crosses the threshold at the first hop and the log-rank comparison has almost nothing left to compare.

The continuous form of the same question does discriminate. Regressing core-finding entailment on generation interacted with a null-result indicator, over all eleven generations of the neutral regime with errors clustered on abstract, gives a baseline decay of $-0.032$ per generation ($p < 10^{-14}$) and an additional $-0.017$ per generation for null-result abstracts ($p = 0.009$). Null findings lose their core claim about $1.5$ times as fast as positive ones, the direction H2 predicted, and the gap persists after the first hop: restricted to generations one through ten, the baseline decay is $-0.0078$ ($p = 0.048$) and the null excess $-0.0145$ ($p = 0.041$). Class trajectories bear this out, with null-result core findings falling from $0.97$ to $0.21$ over ten generations, against $0.37$ for observational and $0.32$ for trial abstracts. We did not preregister this test. It is uncorrected, it supplements a preregistered test that came out null, and it deserves replication before it carries weight.

One chain shows the shape of the loss. A null-result abstract on subclinical hypothyroidism and dyslipidemia in children reports five p-values and closes by saying the association ``still seems to be inconsistent based on the results of this and previous studies''. The first neutral summary keeps none of the five p-values, and core-finding entailment falls from $0.997$ to $0.010$ in that single hop, though the summary still concedes, ``given the inconsistency with previous findings'', that the question is open. By the tenth generation the p-values are still absent, the concession is gone, and what remains is ``the study concluded that there is no correlation between subclinical hypothyroidism and dyslipidemia in children'' followed by a generic call for further research. Note that the chain never overstated the claim in the usual direction, since it stays a null. It removed, almost all at the first hop, the evidence and the acknowledgment that the evidence conflicts, which is what a reader needs in order to weigh a null result.

\subsection{Temperature sensitivity}

Rerunning a stratified 20-abstract subset through all three models at temperature $0.7$ reproduces every sign and nearly every magnitude: hedge density $+0.0515$ against $+0.0527$ for greedy decoding on the same abstracts, numeric fidelity $-0.0511$ against $-0.0399$, qualifier retention $-0.0546$ against $-0.0443$, and causal strength null in both. Information loss is slightly steeper under sampling. Hedge density keeps its effect size and loses significance ($p = 0.09$) on the smaller subset, a power difference with the estimate unchanged. Greedy decoding does not produce the drift.

\subsection{From per-step rates to consumed claims}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig3_composition.pdf}
\caption{Composing measured drift along real intermediation depths. Left: consumption-weighted depth distribution estimated from OpenAlex forward-citation paths over 30 seed works. Right: expected retention at depth under measured neutral-regime rates, with the $0.5$ calibration floor marked.}
\label{fig:depth}
\end{figure}

A forward-citation walk over OpenAlex, starting from our seed abstracts and weighting each reached work by its own citation count, yields 2{,}327 consumption-weighted samples and puts the median intermediation depth of a consumed claim at two hops, with a 90th percentile of four. Composing the measured neutral-regime rates along this distribution, a claim at the median depth of two hops retains $87\%$ of its original qualifiers, the expectation over the whole depth distribution is $86\%$, and qualifier retention stays above a $0.5$ calibration floor out to ten hops. Hedge density rises with depth, so its per-step ratio exceeds one and no depth threshold bounds it from above.

These composed figures are milder than the raw trajectories because of the front-loading result. Depth matters less than the first hop. The first hop dominates, so the marginal cost of the second and third hops that typify real consumption is small, and the danger lies in whether the text a reader receives passed through any unassisted summarization, whatever its depth in the citation graph. Two caveats bound this analysis: it treats a citation hop as a summarization event, which is an approximation, and it rests on 30 seed works.

\section{Instrument validation and a spurious null}
\label{sec:validation}

Our first entailment run produced a clean H2 result, a median half-life of exactly one generation in every claim class in both regimes. It was an artifact.

A simple check caught it (does a source abstract entail a sentence copied out of itself?). Scoring each source against its own core-finding sentence returned a mean entailment of $0.056$, a dismal figure for a quantity that should sit near one, so the instrument was reporting almost nothing as supported, every chain crossed the erosion threshold immediately, and the classes became indistinguishable by construction. Truncation does not explain it, since the median premise is 287 tokens against the model's 512-token limit; the cause was the model itself, because a sentence-pair NLI cross-encoder is trained on single-sentence premises and mislabels document-length ones.

Following \citet{laban2022summac}, we replaced whole-document scoring with granular aggregation, comparing each hypothesis sentence against every premise sentence and keeping the best-supporting one, which raised self-entailment to $0.944$ with the same model. All entailment numbers in this paper come from the corrected instrument, and the scoring pipeline now aborts if self-entailment falls below $0.80$.

We report this at length because the failure mode generalizes. A saturating instrument still produces well-formed survival curves and a publishable null, so any study that thresholds a model-derived score and compares groups can fail this way, and a self-consistency check costs one line and catches it. Backward entailment falls for any summary, because summaries drop content by design, so it measures compression as much as infidelity; forward entailment is the cleaner drift signal, and it falls from $0.97$ to $0.49$ under neutral prompting against $0.97$ to $0.78$ under conservative.

\section{Discussion}

Repeated summarization blurs scientific claims. Across three model families, ten generations, and two prompting regimes, it strips the numbers and the scope conditions that make a claim checkable while sparing the hedges, so the surviving text reads as more tentative, and it leaves causal language untouched. A finding that began as a bounded quantitative statement about a specified population arrives as a hedged directional gesture. This failure differs from the exaggeration documented for press releases, and in one respect it is worse, because a reader who checks an overstated claim against the source will notice the discrepancy, while a vague claim gives the check nothing to grip.

The front-loading result redirects where effort should go. If loss compounded with depth, the intervention would be structural, shortening the chains between primary literature and readers. Because $79\%$ of numeric loss occurs at the first hop, the intervention is local: preserve the numbers and qualifiers through the transition from source document to first machine summary. Conservative prompting, which cuts numeric loss by $80\%$ at no cost in length, is one cheap version of that intervention, and the fact that a prompt achieves it suggests the models have the capacity to keep the information and drop it by default.

Beyond the specific numbers, the study argues for a method. Epistemic properties of AI-mediated communication are measurable with instruments anyone can run, and system-level consequences follow from laboratory rates by composition along measured usage structure, so debates about what AI does to science can proceed on quantities. The same method requires validating instruments before trusting them, as Section~\ref{sec:validation} shows, and letting a preregistered hypothesis fail in public, as ours did.

\textbf{Limitations.} Our chains are homogeneous, one model talking to itself, while real pipelines mix models, humans, and formats; the released harness accepts arbitrary chain compositions, and mixed chains are the natural next experiment. The three models are open-weight and 3.8--7B parameters, so we can't say how frontier proprietary models behave, and hedging in particular plausibly tracks instruction-tuning style. Rule-based instruments measure surface markers of epistemic stance rather than beliefs, and although the entailment arm guards the construct, a determined skeptic can read our effects as stylistic; we reply that consumed style is what downstream readers calibrate on. The depth distribution treats citation hops as summarization events and rests on 30 seed works, which makes the composition analysis the softest quantitative claim in the paper. Abstracts are not full papers, and drift measured on abstracts may differ from drift on sections. Models also improve, so the measured rates are a 2026 snapshot and the harness will outlast the numbers.

\section{Reproducibility}

Everything needed to recompute every number in this paper is in the anonymized repository at \texttt{[ANONYMIZED REPOSITORY URL]}, and we will release it publicly upon acceptance. The release contains the 60-abstract corpus with stratum labels and the 20-abstract sensitivity subset; all 4{,}200 generations as JSONL, one file per model; the full per-generation score table; the hypothesis outputs with the estimator recorded per test; a script, \texttt{src/paper\_numbers.py}, that recomputes every figure quoted in the running text from the released scores and chains; the OpenAlex depth distribution and the composition output; the three figures and the results table; and the time-stamped preregistration with its deviations section. Chain generation ran on a single Kaggle T4 in roughly five hours per model; every other stage runs on a laptop CPU in minutes. The instrument test suite, including the self-entailment gate described in Section~\ref{sec:validation}, runs with \texttt{python tests/test\_instruments.py} and \texttt{python tests/test\_regressions.py}.

The preregistration records two deviations: the estimator fallback described in Section~\ref{sec:design}, and the substitution of Phi-3.5-mini-instruct for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct described in the footnote there.

\section{Conclusion}

We asked whether AI summarization of science drifts with a direction, preregistered the answer that it drifts toward certainty, and found the opposite: hedge density rises, causal language holds still, and precision collapses, with most of the collapse in the first hop. We would like to see the same harness run on mixed human-and-model chains and on at least one frontier model, and to learn whether the front-loading holds when the source is a full paper rather than an abstract. The rates will have moved by the time anyone replicates them, which is why the harness matters more than the numbers we report.
