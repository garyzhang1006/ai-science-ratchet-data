\title{The Vagueness Ratchet: Repeated AI Summarization Makes\\Scientific Claims Less Precise, Not More Confident}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Scientific claims now reach many of their readers through language models, and iterative LLM processing is known to degrade information in general text. Single-step LLM summaries of science already overgeneralize, but whether repeated summarization drifts with a direction, and where along the chain the loss falls, has not been asked. Unbiased noise averages out across a literature, whereas a systematic drift compounds. We preregistered the prediction that summarization chains push claims toward certainty, with fewer hedges and harder causal language, then measured it on 360 chains of depth ten built from 60 stratified PubMed abstracts, three open-weight models, and two prompt regimes. We find instead that hedge density rises by $0.061$ per 100 words per generation ($p = 6.9 \times 10^{-8}$), against the preregistered direction, and the reversal holds in all three models with no sign flips, while causal-claim strength does not move ($p = 0.70$). Precision falls instead: exact retention of effect sizes, intervals, and p-values drops $0.046$ per generation and scope-qualifier retention drops $0.049$ per generation, both at $p < 10^{-39}$, so claims end up \emph{unfalsifiable} rather than overstated. The loss also arrives early: one summarization destroys $41\%$ of a paper's reported numbers, which is $79\%$ of everything lost over ten generations. The depth of a citation chain therefore matters much less than whether any unassisted summarization happened at all. A prompt that demands every caveat be preserved cuts the numeric loss rate by $80\%$ while staying within the source length. We release the chains, code, instruments, and measurements.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A scientific finding now often reaches the person who acts on it through a language model, and sometimes through several: one model summarizes the paper for a review, and another compresses that review into the paragraph a decision-maker reads. \citet{mohamed2025telephone} measured what this costs on news and narrative text, where facts mutate and information degrades under repeated processing. \citet{peters2025generalization} showed that a single LLM summary already broadens a study's conclusions beyond its evidence. Neither line of work asks whether the loss on scientific claims runs in a particular direction as summaries are summarized again. This is our question: \emph{does repeated AI summarization strip hedges, harden causal language, and erase null results faster than symmetric noise would?}

Noise and bias behave differently once they aggregate across a literature. Symmetric perturbation leaves scientific communication roughly intact, since errors decorrelate across chains and readers and the redundancy of a literature absorbs them. A consistent push in one direction is a ratchet. Each hop keeps what the last one took, so a per-step bias that looks negligible on its own compounds at the depths scholarly communication already reaches. We expected the second case, since \citet{bartlett1932remembering} showed that human serial reproduction conventionalizes and sharpens material toward schema-congruent forms, and instruction-tuned models learn from human preferences that reward clear, confident prose, so we preregistered that expectation.

That prediction did not survive the data. Hedges grow denser per hundred words under repeated summarization in all three models we tested, and causal language holds still. The loss lands on precision, as numbers and scope conditions fall away generation after generation while the hedges survive. An abstract that opened with a bounded quantitative statement about a specific population arrives ten hops later as a hedged directional gesture with half its numbers and half its scope conditions gone, so a certainty ratchet is the wrong name for what these pipelines do.

The shape of the loss also came out wrong. Degradation is \emph{front-loaded}: the first summarization destroys $41\%$ of the numeric content of an abstract and accounts for $79\%$ of all numeric loss over ten generations, after which the curve flattens. This inverts the obvious policy response, since provenance rules that shorten citation chains buy much less than a compounding model predicts. The damage is already done at hop one, and a reader is better served by knowing whether the text in front of them passed through any unassisted summarization.

Anyone can rerun our scoring, since it is automated and deterministic, and we never had to lean on a single human annotator the way most integrity work does. We contribute (1) the first measurement of the \emph{direction} of LLM summarization drift on scientific claims, with preregistered hypotheses and automated scoring, (2) the finding that the direction is toward vagueness, replicated across three model families, and (3) the finding that loss is front-loaded, which changes where an intervention should bind. We also release a corpus of 4{,}200 scored generations and document an instrument failure, caught by a self-entailment check, that would otherwise have produced a clean, spurious null.

\section{Related work}

Iterative generation distorts text in measurable ways: \citet{mohamed2025telephone} ran chains of translation and paraphrase through open-weight models and documented cumulative factual drift, its dependence on language and chain design, and its partial mitigation by constrained prompting. \citet{shumailov2024collapse} showed the training-side analogue, model collapse under recursively generated data. However, neither scores the epistemic direction of the movement on scientific material, which is our object. Serial reproduction in humans, from Bartlett's original experiments onward~\citep{bartlett1932remembering} to the iterated-learning laboratory of \citet{kirby2008cumulative}, shows systematic conventionalization and sharpening, and our design is the LLM analogue of that tradition with instruments in place of annotators. Our result runs the other way. Human serial reproduction sharpens the material; ours blurs it.

On the science-communication side, \citet{sumner2014exaggeration} found that exaggerated causal claims in health news track exaggeration already present in the academic press release, and \citet{yavchitz2012misrepresentation} traced spin in press releases to the trial's own abstract, a human baseline our pipeline might have automated. Closest to our question, \citet{peters2025generalization} compared 4{,}900 single-step LLM summaries with their sources and found that most models broadened the scope of the conclusions, nearly five times as often as human summaries did. We ask what happens to scope, hedging, and numbers over repeated summarization and where along the chain the loss falls. Hedging has quantitative traditions in applied linguistics and a shared task in NLP~\citep{farkas2010conll}; we take our hedge list straight from \citet{hyland2005metadiscourse}. \citet{liang2024mapping} and \citet{kobak2024delving} estimated that LLMs already touch a material share of scientific text, which is why a per-step bias here carries population-level consequences.

\section{Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Source claims and stratification}

We drew sixty abstracts from the PubMed open-access subset and stratified them equally into three claim classes. These are randomized trials whose abstracts report at least one numeric effect size with an interval, observational studies with unnegated association language, and explicit null results, operationalized as a null phrase (``no significant difference'', ``did not differ'', ``was not associated'', and similar) appearing in the final third of the abstract. Assignment is mutually exclusive with null-priority, so an abstract whose conclusion reports a null result is classed null regardless of which query surfaced it. Without that rule, fetch order silently absorbs null-result trials into the trial stratum. We keep abstracts of 150--450 words.

\subsection{Chains and regimes}

For each abstract we generate chains of depth ten under every combination of three models (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Phi-3.5-mini-instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3) and two prompt regimes. The \emph{neutral} prompt reads, in full, ``Summarize the following scientific text faithfully, at approximately its original length.'' The \emph{conservative} prompt appends ``Preserve every hedge, qualifier, number, and statement of uncertainty exactly as expressed; do not state any claim more strongly than the original does.'' This gives 360 chains, 180 per regime, and 3{,}600 scored generations. The generation budget is $1.5$ times the input length, capped at 1{,}024 tokens, so a summary can run longer than its source before the cap binds. In what follows, generation and hop are used interchangeably for one summarization step. Decoding is greedy, to separate systematic drift from sampling variance; a sensitivity arm reruns a stratified 20-abstract subset through all three models at temperature $0.7$ with a fixed seed, adding 600 further generations. We ran every chain on a single Kaggle T4 with 4-bit NF4 quantization, at between four and seven hours per model.\footnote{We preregistered Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct as the third model. The Hugging Face token for that gated model never reached the Kaggle kernel, because an account-level secret is not attached to a notebook unless the notebook opts in, so the harness fell back to Phi-3.5-mini-instruct as its fallback table specifies. We noticed only after the Phi run had finished and kept the ungated set, which needs no gated-model token to replicate.}

\subsection{Instruments}

Every generation is scored by five instruments, all deterministic given released code. Hedge density counts matches against a 101-item hedge list drawn from the hedges category of Hyland's metadiscourse appendix~\citep{hyland2005metadiscourse}, normalized per 100 words.\footnote{Word counts throughout use the hedge instrument's tokenizer, which ignores pure numerals and punctuation tokens, so they run a little under a whitespace count.} Causal strength grades the strongest claim sentence on a five-point scale from no-relation to unqualified causation. Negation is scoped to clause boundaries, so that a safety clause such as ``there were no dropouts'' can't invert the efficacy claim that follows it. For numeric fidelity, effect sizes, confidence intervals, p-values, and percentages are extracted by pattern and scored for exact retention against the source, excluding the confidence level itself so that preserving the boilerplate ``95\% CI'' earns no credit. For qualifier retention, population, dosage and study-design phrases are tracked by token overlap. Entailment, which tracks summary faithfulness better than overlap metrics do~\citep{maynez2020faithfulness}, scores each generation against the source bidirectionally with \texttt{cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-base}, a public natural language inference (NLI) model that estimates how strongly one text supports another. Core-finding entailment scores the source's strongest claim sentence against each generation with the same model. That last instrument produced a false result on its first run, and Section~\ref{sec:validation} reports what went wrong. A positive control confirms that the rule-based instruments move when the text does. When associational phrasing in the 60 sources is rewritten as unqualified causation, mean causal strength rises from $3.07$ to $4.23$ with no abstract moving the wrong way. When the unqualified causal sentences are hedged it falls to $2.62$, and when every hedge is deleted hedge density goes to zero.

\subsection{Analysis}

The estimand for H1 is the mean adjacent-generation change in each marker. We specified a mixed model with a random intercept per source abstract and a cluster-robust ordinary least squares (OLS) fallback. In practice the fallback fired in every cell. Differencing removes the abstract-level intercept by construction and leaves no level variance for a random intercept to absorb. All reported standard errors are therefore clustered on the source abstract (60 clusters), which carries the dependence among the ten differences within a chain, and all tests are two-sided. For most markers there are 1{,}800 such differences per regime; for numeric fidelity there are 1{,}560, since abstracts with no extractable statistic are dropped, and for qualifier retention 1{,}710. We Holm-correct across the five markers within each regime. Our three preregistered hypotheses are (1) H1, that every marker drifts toward certainty, (2) H2, that the core finding of a null-result abstract erodes faster than that of a positive one, and (3) H3, that conservative prompting damps every rate without flipping a sign. We fixed all hypotheses, model specifications, and exclusion rules in a time-stamped preregistration in the repository.

Table~\ref{tab:h1} gives the per-step effects. We find that hedge density rises by $0.061$ per 100 words per generation under neutral prompting (95\% CI $0.039$ to $0.083$, $p = 6.9 \times 10^{-8}$), the opposite of the preregistered direction, and causal-claim strength does not move ($+0.0067$, $p = 0.70$). That instrument takes the strongest sentence in a text, so it reports a ceiling and misses any loosening around it. Over ten generations, mean hedge density climbs from $0.72$ to $1.33$ per 100 words, an $85\%$ increase, and neither marker behaves as a certainty ratchet requires.

The rise is in the share of words that are hedges. The raw number of hedges per summary is flat, at $-0.018$ per generation ($p = 0.25$), while neutral summaries shrink from 210 words to 116, so the hedges that survive occupy a larger share of a smaller text. The models shed numbers and qualifiers faster than they shed hedges. We checked whether compression alone explains the rise: regressing the per-step change in density on the per-step change in word count leaves a residual density increase of $+0.045$ per generation ($p < 10^{-3}$), so it does not. 

Nor, in a post hoc check, is the rise a matter of rounding words. Hyland's list includes approximators such as ``about'' and ``approximately'' and frequency words such as ``often''. When every item of that kind is dropped, the density of epistemic hedges (``may'', ``could'', ``suggests'', ``indicate'') still rises from $0.51$ to $0.91$ per 100 words, $+0.040$ per generation ($p = 1 \times 10^{-5}$), and the approximators add the remaining third of the total rise.

In each model separately, hedge density rises, with no sign flip in any of the three (uncorrected $p$): Qwen2.5-7B $+0.0499$ ($p = 4.0 \times 10^{-4}$), Phi-3.5-mini $+0.0969$ ($p = 3.1 \times 10^{-6}$), Mistral-7B $+0.0366$ ($p = 2.0 \times 10^{-4}$). Phi, the smallest of the three, shows the largest rise.

In contrast, the precision of the claim degrades. Exact retention of effect sizes, intervals, and p-values falls $0.046$ per generation, scope-qualifier retention falls $0.049$ per generation, and bidirectional entailment against the source falls $0.068$ per generation, all at $p < 10^{-39}$. In the neutral regime, pooled numeric fidelity drops from $0.88$ to $0.42$ and qualifier retention from $0.98$ to $0.49$ across ten generations. By the end of a chain a claim keeps about half its numbers and half its scope conditions, at roughly double the hedge density it started with. The surviving claim is vague: too imprecise to check, though it no longer overstates the evidence either.

\subsection{The first hop does most of the damage}

Figure~\ref{fig:drift} shows a shape we did not anticipate when we framed the ratchet. Numeric fidelity falls from $0.88$ to $0.52$ between the source and generation one, a loss of $41\%$ of the original content that accounts for $79\%$ of all numeric loss across the full ten generations. We see the same shape in qualifier retention, where the first hop accounts for $52\%$ of total loss. The curves then flatten. Whatever survives one summarization tends to survive nine more.

For anyone designing interventions this shape counts for more than the direction result. Under exponential compounding, shortening chains would be the lever. In the measured data the dominant risk sits at the transition from the source document to any machine summary of it, and further hops add little. Cutting a pipeline from four hops to two moves numeric fidelity from $0.45$ to $0.49$, about three points of the original numbers, because most of them were gone after hop one. A policy that keeps a reader one hop from the source, or preserves its numbers through the first hop, captures most of the benefit.

\subsection{Conservative prompting damps four of five rates and stays within the source length}

Instructing the model to preserve every hedge, qualifier, number, and statement of uncertainty reduces the drift magnitude of four of the five markers without flipping any sign. H3 holds for those four, and the damping is not small. Causal strength, null in both regimes, drifts slightly further under the conservative prompt, and that difference is itself null ($p = 0.66$). Numeric loss falls by $79.7\%$ ($p = 9.8 \times 10^{-47}$ on the interaction), qualifier loss by $76.9\%$ ($p = 1.6 \times 10^{-27}$), entailment loss by $58.9\%$, and the hedge-density rise by $45.7\%$ ($p = 0.012$ raw, $0.024$ after Holm); all four survive Holm correction across the five markers. At generation ten, numeric fidelity of $0.79$ is retained under conservative prompting against $0.42$ under neutral, and qualifier retention of $0.86$ against $0.49$.

The price is paid in compression: conservative summaries average 195 words against 123 for neutral ones, from a 210-word source, so preserving the science means giving up most of the shortening while staying within the source length. However, each of those four rates remains significantly nonzero under the conservative prompt, so prompting leaves the problem in place; it is a large effect available at no computational cost to anyone who controls the prompt.

\subsection{Null results erode fastest under the continuous test}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.80\linewidth]{fig2_survival.pdf}
\caption{Survival of the core finding under repeated summarization, by claim class and regime, with the erosion event defined as core-finding entailment falling below $0.5$. The neutral regime saturates at the first generation, which is why the preregistered log-rank comparison across classes has little room to discriminate.}
\label{fig:halflife}
\end{figure}

The preregistered H2 test, the first generation at which core-finding entailment drops below $0.5$, censored at generation ten and compared across claim classes by log-rank, is null in both regimes (neutral $p = 0.40$, conservative $p = 0.21$). The binary test saturates, and Figure~\ref{fig:halflife} shows why: under neutral prompting the median half-life is a single generation for every class, so nearly every chain crosses the threshold at the first hop and the log-rank comparison has almost nothing left to compare.

The continuous form of the same question does discriminate. For this we regress core-finding entailment on generation interacted with a null-result indicator, over all eleven generations of the neutral regime with errors clustered on abstract, and obtain a baseline decay of $-0.032$ per generation ($p < 10^{-14}$) and an additional $-0.017$ per generation for null-result abstracts ($p = 0.009$). Null findings lose their core claim about $1.5$ times as fast as positive ones, the direction H2 predicted. The gap persists after the first hop: restricted to generations one through ten, the baseline decay is $-0.0078$ ($p = 0.048$) and the null excess $-0.0145$ ($p = 0.041$). Class trajectories bear this out, with null-result core findings falling from $0.97$ to $0.21$ over ten generations, against $0.37$ for observational and $0.32$ for trial abstracts. We did not preregister this test, it is uncorrected, and it deserves replication before it carries weight.

One chain shows the shape of the loss. A null-result abstract on subclinical hypothyroidism and dyslipidemia in children reports five p-values and closes by saying the association ``still seems to be inconsistent based on the results of this and previous studies''. The first neutral summary keeps none of the five p-values, and core-finding entailment falls from $0.997$ to $0.010$ in that single hop, though the summary still concedes, ``given the inconsistency with previous findings'', that the question is open. By the tenth generation the p-values are still absent, the concession is gone, and what remains is ``the study concluded that there is no correlation between subclinical hypothyroidism and dyslipidemia in children'' followed by a generic call for further research. The chain never overstates the claim in the usual sense, since it stays a null throughout. It removed, almost all at the first hop, the evidence and the acknowledgment that the evidence conflicts, which is what a reader needs to weigh a null result.

\subsection{Temperature sensitivity}

Rerunning a stratified 20-abstract subset through all three models at temperature $0.7$ reproduces the sign of all four rule-based markers and nearly their magnitudes. We did not rescore entailment on this arm, so it carries no sensitivity estimate. Hedge density drifts $+0.0515$ against $+0.0527$ for greedy decoding on the same abstracts, numeric fidelity $-0.0511$ against $-0.0399$, qualifier retention $-0.0546$ against $-0.0443$, and causal strength is null in both. Information loss is slightly steeper under sampling, and hedge density keeps its effect size while losing significance after Holm correction on the smaller subset ($p = 0.09$).

\subsection{From per-step rates to consumed claims}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.80\linewidth]{fig3_composition.pdf}
\caption{Composing measured drift along real intermediation depths. Left: consumption-weighted depth distribution estimated from OpenAlex forward-citation paths seeded by all 60 source abstracts. Right: retention at each depth read off the measured neutral-regime trajectories, relative to the source, with the $0.5$ floor marked.}
\label{fig:depth}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:depth} composes the per-step rates along real intermediation depths. The depth distribution comes from a forward-citation walk over OpenAlex~\citep{priem2022openalex} seeded by all 60 source abstracts, following at most three citing works per node to a depth of four, preferring reviews, and weighting each reached work by its own citation count. The walk yields 4{,}920 consumption-weighted samples and puts the median intermediation depth of a consumed claim at two hops, with a 90th percentile of three. We read retention at each depth off the measured neutral-regime trajectory. A fitted constant per-hop rate would be wrong, because the loss is front-loaded: the geometric rate that matches qualifier retention over the full chain predicts $0.87$ survival at two hops, where the measured curve gives $0.62$. Depth here counts citation hops, which we treat as summarization events, and human citation is not the same process as repeated paraphrase. A claim at the median depth of two hops retains $62\%$ of its original qualifiers, and the expectation over the whole depth distribution is $66\%$. Qualifier retention stays above a $0.5$ floor through all ten measured hops, though only just ($0.50$ at hop ten). Hedge density at the median depth is $1.44$ times its source value, and two thirds of null-result chains have lost their core finding by then.

Because the first hop dominates, the second and third hops that typify real consumption add little beyond it: $74\%$ of qualifiers survive hop one and $62\%$ survive hop two. Depth in the citation graph turns out to matter less than we expected going in, and what matters is whether even one summarization step happened without supervision. The walk is also branch-limited, so it samples the citation graph rather than enumerating it.

\section{Instrument validation and a spurious null}
\label{sec:validation}

Our first entailment run produced a clean H2 result, a median half-life of exactly one generation in every claim class in both regimes, and it was an artifact.

The check that caught it asks whether a source abstract entails a sentence copied out of itself. When each source was scored against its own core-finding sentence, the mean entailment was $0.056$, a dismal figure for a quantity that should sit near one. The instrument was reporting almost nothing as supported, so every chain crossed the erosion threshold at the first hop and the classes became indistinguishable by construction. Truncation does not explain it. The median premise is 287 tokens against the model's 512-token limit, and the cause was the model itself, because a sentence-pair NLI cross-encoder is trained on single-sentence premises and mislabels document-length ones.

Following \citet{laban2022summac}, we replaced whole-document scoring with granular aggregation, comparing each hypothesis sentence against every premise sentence and keeping the best-supporting one, which raised self-entailment to $0.944$ with the same model. All entailment numbers in this paper come from the corrected instrument, and the scoring pipeline now aborts if self-entailment falls below $0.80$, a gate we set before rescoring.

This one is worth dwelling on. Any study that thresholds a model-derived score and compares groups across it can saturate the same way, producing a clean survival curve and a publishable null out of an instrument that has quietly stopped discriminating. A self-consistency check costs one line and would have caught ours sooner. Backward entailment falls for any summary, because summaries drop content by design, so it measures compression as much as infidelity. Forward entailment is the cleaner drift signal, and it falls from $0.97$ to $0.49$ under neutral prompting against $0.97$ to $0.78$ under conservative.

\section{Discussion}

We find that repeated summarization blurs scientific claims. Across three model families, ten generations, and two prompting regimes, it strips the numbers and the scope conditions that make a claim checkable while sparing the hedges, so the surviving text reads as more tentative, while the strongest causal sentence holds its grade. This failure differs from the exaggeration documented for press releases, and in one respect it is worse. An overstated claim can be caught by checking it against the source. A vague one offers nothing to check.

The front-loading result, however, redirects where effort should go. If loss compounded with depth, the intervention would be structural, shortening the chains between primary literature and readers. Because $79\%$ of numeric loss occurs at the first hop, the intervention is local: preserve the numbers and qualifiers through the transition from source document to first machine summary. Conservative prompting, which cuts numeric loss by $80\%$ without exceeding the source length, is one cheap version of that intervention. The models can clearly keep this information, since a prompt gets them to, and they drop it anyway when nobody asks.

Beyond the specific numbers, the study shows that the epistemic properties of AI-mediated communication can be measured with instruments anyone can run, and that composing laboratory rates along a measured usage distribution turns a per-step bias into an estimate of what readers receive.

\textbf{Limitations.} Our chains are homogeneous, one model talking to itself, while real pipelines mix models, humans, and formats; the released harness accepts arbitrary chain compositions, and mixed chains are the natural next experiment. The three models are open weight and 3.8--7B parameters, so we can't say how frontier proprietary models behave, and hedging in particular plausibly tracks instruction-tuning style. Rule-based instruments measure surface markers of epistemic stance rather than beliefs, and although the entailment arm guards the construct, a determined skeptic can read our effects as stylistic; we reply that consumed style is what downstream readers calibrate on. The depth distribution treats citation hops as summarization events and comes from a branch-limited walk seeded by 60 abstracts, which makes the composition analysis the softest quantitative claim in the paper. Abstracts are not full papers, and drift measured on abstracts may differ from drift on sections; the abstracts are also all biomedical, and fields with different hedging conventions may drift differently. Sixty source abstracts is small for the class comparison in H2, and that is where power ran out. Exact matching counts a rounded number as lost, so the numeric rates are floors, and the entailment arm rests on a single NLI model, corrected as described but never cross-checked against a second. Models also improve, so the measured rates describe the 2026 systems we ran and will need remeasuring on their successors.

\section{Reproducibility}

Everything needed to recompute every number in this paper is in the anonymized repository at \texttt{[ANONYMIZED REPOSITORY URL]}, and we will release it publicly upon acceptance. The release contains the corpus with stratum labels, all 4{,}200 generations as JSONL, the per-generation score table, the hypothesis outputs with the estimator recorded per test, the depth distribution, the composition and positive-control outputs, the figures, and the time-stamped preregistration with its deviations section. A script (\texttt{src/paper\_numbers.py}) recomputes every figure quoted in the running text. Chain generation and NLI scoring ran on a single Kaggle T4, four to seven hours per model; the instruments, analysis, composition, and figures run on a laptop CPU in minutes. The instrument test suite, including the self-entailment gate described in Section~\ref{sec:validation}, runs with \texttt{python tests/test\_instruments.py} and \texttt{python tests/test\_regressions.py}.

Two diagnostic figures, the $0.056$ self-entailment mean and the 287-token median premise, come from the discarded pre-fix run and survive only in the release notes. The preregistration records two deviations, the estimator fallback and the Phi-3.5 substitution, both described in Section~\ref{sec:design}. Appendix~\ref{app:practical} lists the implementation decisions that cost the most debugging time.

\section{Conclusion}

We preregistered that summarization chains would drift toward certainty, and the chains did the opposite. Hedge density went up and precision fell apart, most of it in the first hop, while the causal measure did not move. The rates will have moved by the time anyone replicates them, which is why the harness matters more than the numbers we report. We would like to see it run on mixed human-and-model chains and on a frontier model, and to learn whether the front-loading holds for full papers.

\appendix
\section{Practical considerations}
\label{app:practical}

Each of the following cost us at least a day, and none would be visible in the results had we not caught it.

\textbf{A class label named ``null''.} Our three strata are \texttt{rct}, \texttt{obs}, and \texttt{null}, and the score table stores the class as a string. \texttt{pandas} treats the string ``null'' as a missing value by default, so every grouped analysis dropped the entire null-result stratum and reported two classes where there were three. The reader now passes \texttt{keep\_default\_na=False} with an explicit list of missing-value tokens, and a regression test reloads the table and asserts that all three class labels survive.

\textbf{Resuming interrupted chains.} A Kaggle session ends after twelve hours whether or not a model has finished its 120 chains. Our first resume logic restarted any incomplete chain from the source abstract, which regenerated the early generations under a different context and appended them as duplicate rows, so a resumed run carried two copies of generations one through $k$ for every chain it touched. Resume is now keyed on the last written generation and its text, so a chain continues from where it stopped, and the scorer also deduplicates on (abstract, model, regime, generation) and warns when it does.

\textbf{Child-process output on Kaggle.} The chain generator runs as a subprocess inside the notebook, and Kaggle drops long subprocess output, so a kernel that was generating normally showed an empty log for hours and looked hung. The notebook now reads the child's stdout line by line and prints each line itself with a flush.

\textbf{Notebook secrets.} A Hugging Face token stored at the account level is not visible to a notebook unless it is attached to that notebook, which is why the preregistered gated model was replaced by its ungated fallback (Section~\ref{sec:design}). The harness now prints the swap when it makes one.

\textbf{Download backend.} Model downloads hung indefinitely on Kaggle with the default Hugging Face transfer backend; setting \texttt{HF\_HUB\_DISABLE\_XET=1} before importing the library fixed it.

\textbf{Document-length premises.} Described in Section~\ref{sec:validation}; the granular scorer splits both premise and hypothesis into sentences, takes the maximum entailment over premise sentences for each hypothesis sentence, and averages over hypothesis sentences, so the 512-token limit is never reached and the self-entailment gate holds at $0.944$.
